## John Tanner — five distinct beats

Source set: [Jared Carter account in the Church student manual](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/doctrine-and-covenants-student-manual-2017/chapter-29-doctrine-and-covenants-77-80?lang=eng); [Joseph Smith Papers biography and documentary links](https://www.josephsmithpapers.org/person/john-tanner); [Leonard J. Arrington, The John Tanner Family](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/1979/03/the-john-tanner-family?lang=eng). Use the current source-reviewed story facts as the prose foundation; the visual details below are compositional proposals, not additional historical claims.

1. **tanner-healing-witness — September 1832.** Tanner rising carefully while a missionary takes his hand, as Carter reported. Show restored movement and attentive witnesses without invented Christ appearance, miraculous light, or dramatic medical injury. If Carter's identity remains unresolved, frame the supporting hand and Tanner's movement without an identifiable second face. Caption attributes healing testimony to Carter.
2. **tanner-journey-kirtland — winter 1834–35.** Family belongings secured for the move, a road and winter landscape leading toward the community. Tanner's family moved after a prompting described in the later family account. Avoid inventing exact weather, a named relative's action, or a recognizable building at a location not documented. This is a migration composition, not another cash scene.
3. **tanner-temple-support — January 1835.** A modest desk, funds and an ordinary folded note beside a view toward the temple works. The $2,000 loan is documented in the story sources; do not make up legible banknotes, a forged historical signature, or the appearance of the original document. If both Tanner and Joseph appear, use locked Joseph reference and a disclosed Tanner reconstruction.
4. **tanner-displacement — Missouri hardship.** Packed household goods and a family preparing to leave, emphasizing the cost and disruption after his Missouri experience. JSP supports moves and persecution; do not fabricate an exact violent encounter, attacker identity, wounds, or a quotation. Use no readable invented eviction order. This scene can safely begin as an environmental composition while identity is resolved.
5. **tanner-debt-forgiven — 1844 remembrance.** An aged Tanner relinquishes the note at a quiet meeting with Joseph, or a close composition of hands releasing the folded paper. The family tradition recounted by Arrington is the source; label as a remembered act, not a photographed/documentarily exact exchange. Distinguish this from the 1835 loan through age, gesture, setting, and the purpose of releasing a debt.

Depicting a generic camp task is interpretive; do not invent a named rescue or official assignment. This expands his life beyond the single conversion encounter.
