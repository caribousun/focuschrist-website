## Source-grounded story notes

### Elijah Able (also spelled Abel)

[Church History Topics biography](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/history/topics/elijah-able?lang=eng): baptism in 1832; elder and Seventy ordinations in 1836; missionary work; family and skilled work; continued priesthood standing despite the later restriction; denied requests for his endowment and sealing. He continued serving and died in 1884. His story should include both his contribution and the blessings withheld from him. Ambrose Palmer ordained him an elder; do not say Joseph Smith personally performed that ordination.

[Joseph Smith Papers biography](https://www.josephsmithpapers.org/person/elijah-able) gives January 25, 1836 for ordination and March 31 for the license, with a link to the actual license. It lists July 25, 1810 as birth date, while the Church History Topics article gives an uncertain 1808–1812 range. Avoid an unnecessary exact birth date rather than silently reconciling differing official summaries. The portrait is dated approximately 1862–1873, likely photographed by Edward Martin; it is not his appearance at his 1836 ordination.

### Jane Elizabeth Manning James

[Church History Topics biography](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/history/topics/jane-elizabeth-manning-james?lang=eng): born free in Connecticut; converted in 1842–43; denied boat passage because of race and walked much of the journey to Nauvoo; lived and worked with the Smith household; migrated west with Isaac James in 1847. Include her labor, Relief Society participation, temple contributions, and repeated petitions for endowment and sealing. Her life was more than an unanswered request.

The article describes her 1888 proxy baptisms and the unique 1894 proxy sealing to Joseph Smith's family **as a servant**. Do not present that as an ordinary family sealing or a satisfactory substitute for what she requested. She did not receive her endowment and family sealings during her lifetime; those ordinances were performed on her behalf in 1979. The article links her 1890 and 1903 letters, which offer her own voice. Avoid invented dialogue or a claim that her initial misunderstanding of Emma's adoption offer explains the later racial restriction.

Documentary leads linked from the article: [1890 letter catalog record](https://catalog.churchofjesuschrist.org/assets/f22ada32-3117-458b-a390-125f38af1d49) and [1903 letter PDF](https://content.churchofjesuschrist.org/chpress/bc/PDF/Documents%20from%20catalog/Jane-E-James-to-Joseph-F-Smith-31-Aug-1903-CHL.pdf). These are leads, not independently transcribed manuscript evidence in this pass; the PDF was unavailable through the browsing extractor.

### Explain the restriction accurately

[Priesthood and Temple Restriction](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/history/topics/priesthood-and-temple-restriction?lang=eng) distinguishes early Black priesthood holders, Brigham Young's public 1852 announcement, and later restriction of endowment and sealing. Say Black men of African descent were restricted from ordination, and Black men and women of African descent from endowment and sealing. Baptism and Church membership remained possible; proxy baptism exceptions mean that “Black members could never enter a temple” is too broad.

[Race and the Priesthood](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/gospel-topics-essays/race-and-the-priesthood?lang=eng) supplies nineteenth-century racial context and explicitly rejects explanations involving divine racial curses, premortal unfaithfulness, racial inferiority, or interracial marriage as sin. Read the contextual paragraphs and the concluding disavowal together. Historical racial culture is relevant context; do not claim that it establishes every cause or that the restriction was expressly commanded by a recorded revelation. Do not imply that people had to become sufficiently faithful to deserve its removal.

### How the 1978 revelation was received

[Official Declaration 2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/dc-testament/od/2?lang=eng) is the central primary record. Read its introduction, the June 8 letter, and the September 30 conference action together. It identifies extended meditation and prayer, the June 1 temple revelation and affirmation, and unanimous acceptance by successive leadership bodies. Preserve four distinct dates: June 1 experience; June 8 letter; June 9 public announcement; September 30 common-consent acceptance. The latter two should not be confused with the date of the revelatory experience. Its introduction says the record does not clearly explain the restriction's origins.

[Witnessing the Faithfulness](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/revelations-in-context/witnessing-the-faithfulness?lang=eng) gives the surrounding human and institutional context: believers in Ghana, the Martins family in Brazil, and Joseph and Toe Freeman in the United States; Kimball's sustained prayer and requests for fellow leaders to study and pray; discussion and prayer on June 1; participants' descriptions of spiritual confirmation. Attribute recollections to the participants. Do not invent an audible voice, visible visitation, or a transcript of private prayer. The Freeman and Martins families provide concrete consequences—ordination and temple sealing—without portraying Black members as passive people merely waiting.

[Official Declaration 2 student manual chapter](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/manual/doctrine-and-covenants-student-manual-2017/chapter-56-official-declaration-2?lang=eng) corroborates the process and announcement sequence. The public declaration extends ordination to worthy men without race restrictions and temple blessings to families; it does not introduce women's priesthood ordination.
