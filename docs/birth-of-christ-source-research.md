# Birth of Christ: verified source map and twenty scene briefs

Research only, 2026-09-19. Official Church scripture pages were opened live; this is not a page implementation, approved image list, or generation request. No images produced. Saved outside the site repository.

## Narrative plan

1. **The promised Son:** begin with Isaiah's Immanuel and royal-child passages, Micah's Bethlehem prophecy, and the distinction between a prediction and its later application. Isaiah 11 contributes Davidic and messianic context, not another manger event.
2. **Witnesses before His birth:** Nephi's vision; Benjamin's report of an angel; Alma's anticipation of Mary's Son and His saving ministry; Samuel's announced signs. Keep these different prophets and centuries distinct.
3. **A people prepared for the Lord:** Luke's account of Zacharias and John, then Gabriel's message to Mary. Explain that John prepares the way; he is not the promised Messiah.
4. **Mary and Joseph respond:** the visit to Elisabeth, Joseph's dream, and the journey to Bethlehem.
5. **The Savior is born:** Luke's manger, shepherds, and later temple witnesses; Matthew's separate account of the wise men and the family's departure to Egypt.
6. **The promised sign in the Book of Mormon:** Nephi's prayer and the fulfillment in 3 Nephi 1. Never place Samuel on the wall during this later fulfillment.
7. **Why His birth matters:** connect John 1:14 with Mosiah 3 and Alma 7: the Son enters mortality to redeem and succor. Avoid sentimental separation of His birth from His whole mission.

## Source map and precise boundaries

### Old Testament

- [Isaiah 7:3–14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/7?id=p3-p14&lang=eng#p3): Isaiah's encounter with Ahaz has its own historical setting; verse 14 announces Immanuel. [Matthew 1:22–23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/1?id=p22-p23&lang=eng#p22) explicitly applies the prophecy to Jesus. Present that textual connection rather than erasing the original setting.
- [Isaiah 9:2, 6–7](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/9?id=p2,p6-p7&lang=eng#p2): light, the promised child, His names, and enduring Davidic rule. These are prophecy, not a report of Isaiah standing beside Mary. A symbolic illustration must be called symbolic.
- [Isaiah 11:1–5](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/11?id=p1-p5&lang=eng#p1): Jesse/Branch imagery, the Spirit, righteous judgment. [11:6–9](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/11?id=p6-p9&lang=eng#p6) portrays peace; it is not evidence of wolves and lions at Jesus' birth. Do not identify every figure or image in the chapter as the infant Jesus.
- [Micah 5:2](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/micah/5?id=p2&lang=eng#p2): Bethlehem Ephratah and the coming ruler. [Matthew 2:4–6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/2?id=p4-p6&lang=eng#p4) supplies the Gospel connection. No speculative precise birth-site reconstruction.
- [Isaiah 40:3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/isa/40?id=p3&lang=eng#p3), [Malachi 3:1](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/ot/mal/3?id=p1&lang=eng#p1): preparation/messenger passages. Compare [John 1:19–23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/1?id=p19-p23&lang=eng#p19), [Matthew 3:1–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/3?id=p1-p3&lang=eng#p1), and [Matthew 11:7–10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/11?id=p7-p10&lang=eng#p7). John the Baptist's adult ministry prepares for Christ's public ministry; it is not an additional birth-night scene.

### New Testament

- [Matthew 1:18–25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/1?id=p18-p25&lang=eng#p18): Joseph's dilemma, an angel in a dream, obedience, Jesus' name, and Emmanuel. The angel is not named Gabriel in this account.
- [Matthew 2:1–15](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/2?id=p1-p15&lang=eng#p1): wise men, star, house, gifts, warning, departure to Egypt. Their number and names, royal titles, camels, and the child's exact age are unstated. Three gifts do not establish three men. Do not combine their visit with Luke's manger/shepherd scene or pretend the Gospels supply a complete day-by-day sequence.
- [Luke 1:5–80](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/1?id=p5-p80&lang=eng#p5): two annunciations and two distinct mothers/sons, with Mary visiting Elisabeth. Read [1:76–79](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/1?id=p76-p79&lang=eng#p76) for John's preparatory mission. An exact date, Mary's numerical age, and the annunciation room's furnishings are not given.
- [Luke 2:1–38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/2?id=p1-p38&lang=eng#p1): travel, birth, shepherds, presentation, Simeon, Anna. Luke does not narrate an innkeeper rejecting the family or specify a donkey, stable architecture, or attending animals.
- [John 1:1–14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/1?id=p1-p14&lang=eng#p1): the Word, life/light, and incarnation; John is a witness to the Light, not that Light. This is theological testimony rather than a second narrative description of the delivery or manger.

### Book of Mormon

- [1 Nephi 11:13–21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/1-ne/11?id=p13-p21&lang=eng#p13): Nephi sees the mother and infant in vision. He is not physically visiting their home. Verse 13 identifies Nazareth in the vision; do not rewrite this as an eyewitness nativity at Bethlehem. The text's appearance language does not establish a modern racial classification.
- [2 Nephi 25:12–19](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/25?id=p12-p19&lang=eng#p12): Nephi's prophetic testimony of the Messiah, His death/resurrection, timing relative to Lehi's departure, and name. Do not convert its chronology into an unsupported modern calendar date.
- [2 Nephi 26:1–3](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/2-ne/26?id=p1-p3&lang=eng#p1): anticipated post-resurrection ministry and signs of birth/death/resurrection. These are distinct occasions.
- [Mosiah 3:2–10](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/mosiah/3?id=p2-p10&lang=eng#p2): Benjamin reports an angel's announcement, names Mary, and connects Christ's coming with redemption. The audience hears Benjamin's report, not a new angel simultaneously speaking over his shoulder.
- [Alma 7:7–13](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/alma/7?id=p7-p13&lang=eng#p7): Alma names Mary and speaks of Christ's birth and succoring mission. Verse 8 explicitly limits what Alma claims to know about a mortal visit among them. Preserve verse 10's wording about Jerusalem as the land of the forefathers; do not silently quote it as Bethlehem or relocate Luke's birth account to Jerusalem city.
- [Helaman 14:2–6](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/hel/14?id=p2-p6&lang=eng#p2): Samuel predicts five years, a night without darkness, and a new star. This is prediction. [Helaman 13:2–4](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/hel/13?id=p2-p4&lang=eng#p2) supplies the wall setting.
- [Helaman 16:1–8, 13–14](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/hel/16?id=p1-p8,p13-p14&lang=eng#p1): responses to Samuel, Nephi's ministry, and preliminary wonders. Its wise men receiving angels are not identified as Matthew's traveling wise men. Samuel leaves after his preaching; do not keep him on the wall years later.
- [3 Nephi 1:9–21](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/bofm/3-ne/1?id=p9-p21&lang=eng#p9): threatened believers, Nephi's day-long prayer, the Lord's voice, and the fulfilled sign/new star. This Nephi is the son of Nephi, not the son of Lehi. The Lord speaks; no bodily appearance to him is described.

## Composition and continuity controls for later work

- This set intentionally includes prophet portraits, architecture/landscape, a vision, a dream, intimate conversations, a travel scene, close infant care, night fields, temple testimony, a household visit, solitary prayer, and a whole-community sign. Avoid twenty variations of a kneeling group around one central figure.
- Mary, Joseph, Gabriel, and the infant need consistent identities across relevant scenes. The various Nephis must not be treated as one person. Any exact age, facial likeness, named supporting person, or clothing detail requires a source or clear interpretive status.
- The vision and dream scenes need enough visual separation that viewers cannot mistake them for bodily visits. Do not visually manufacture the mechanism of divine conception.
- Retain the page's source distinctions in captions and study panels. A synthesis can connect witnesses without claiming that different texts describe the same cast, building, or instant.
- Do not claim December 25, a precise year, a night of winter snow, an astronomical explanation of the star, or modern archaeological certainty from these passages.
- No new artwork has been generated or approved. Source-grounded subject selection is only the first gate; images, visible captions, study panels, and linked verses require independent review afterward.

## Appearance uncertainty for recurring figures

The reviewed scripture passages do not supply authenticated facial likenesses for Mary, Joseph, Gabriel, Simeon, or Anna. Consistent faces may support narrative continuity, but they remain artistic interpretations; actor casting in a Church film is not evidence of historical likeness. An official Church article explicitly states that Mary's age at the annunciation is unknown: [Gaye Strathearn, “Mary, the Mother of Jesus,” January 2019](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/liahona/2019/01/mary-the-mother-of-jesus?lang=eng).

- **Mary and Joseph:** Luke 1:26–38 and Matthew 1:18–25 establish their relationship and actions, not precise ages or facial features. Avoid asserting a precise teenage age or an elderly Joseph from tradition alone.
- **Gabriel:** Luke 1:11, 19, 26 names the messenger to Zacharias and Mary. The text gives no facial description or wings. Matthew 1:20 leaves Joseph's dream messenger unnamed; do not identify him as Gabriel as a scriptural fact.
- **Simeon:** Luke 2:25–35 describes a just, devout man led by the Spirit who holds Jesus; it does not explicitly call him a priest or specify his age. An older artistic portrayal is interpretive, not a textual requirement.
- **Anna:** Luke 2:36–38 identifies a prophetess of great age and widowhood. An elderly woman is textually supported; avoid resolving the wording about eighty-four years into an exact age claim unnecessarily.

These limits leave room for historically plausible clothing and ordinary human expressions while keeping exact physical identities clearly interpretive. Verified 2026-09-19.
