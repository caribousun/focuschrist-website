# Birth and two study additions: historical accuracy review

2026-09-19. Bounded independent review of the 23 Birth titles, captions, alt text and scripture links in `birth-of-christ-art-review.json`, their source and visual review documents, plus the Paul/Athens and Jesus/Bethany additions. This review distinguishes recorded events from plausible artistic choices; it does not authenticate ancient faces, reconstruct exact buildings, or replace owner acceptance. The approved Christ portrait remains protected and unchanged.

**Result:** No new source contradiction requiring an image or visitor-copy change was found in the reviewed scope. The following evidence limits must remain attached to the work. Prior fixes to the Visitation pregnancy, birth-sign night, angel identity and manger continuity are recorded separately in the artwork review; this report concerns current selections.

## Ages and identities

- **Mary:** A youthful adult appearance is an artistic interpretation. Do not state a specific teenage age or numerical age. The Church-published article explicitly says her age at the annunciation is unknown. Its historical discussion supports modest rural Nazareth surroundings, while stating archaeological discoveries cannot be tied directly to Mary's family. [Gaye Strathearn, Mary, the Mother of Jesus](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/liahona/2019/01/mary-the-mother-of-jesus?lang=eng).
- **Joseph:** Matthew identifies his relationship, dream and actions, but supplies no age or facial description. The current adult portrayal is not contradicted; neither a large age gap nor a youthful exact age should become a historical claim. His dream angel is unnamed, so do not identify that figure as Gabriel. [Matthew 1:18–25](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/1?id=p18-p25&lang=eng#p18).
- **Zacharias and Elisabeth:** Advanced age is explicit. Current gray-haired depictions in scenes 07, 09 and 21 fit that category. No numerical ages are established. Gabriel is named in the announcement to Zacharias and to Mary; his depicted facial likeness is interpretive. [Luke 1:5–19, 26–38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/1?id=p5-p19,p26-p38&lang=eng#p5).
- **Simeon:** The narrative does not specify his age or call him a priest. An older portrayal is permissible interpretation, not a textual requirement. His words to Mary fall in verses 34–35, included in the current metadata's expanded range. [Luke 2:25–35](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/2?id=p25-p35&lang=eng#p25).
- **Anna:** The text establishes great age and widowhood. Avoid asserting that she was exactly 84: the wording about eighty-four years should not be resolved into a precise age in a caption. The current elderly portrayal and unspecified age avoid that problem. [Luke 2:36–38](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/luke/2?id=p36-p38&lang=eng#p36).
- **Jesus at the wise men's visit:** Matthew gives a house, the child with Mary, worship and gifts; no exact child age or number of visitors. Herod's order concerning children two and under is not a stated birthday for Jesus. An infant/young child depiction is an artistic choice, not a contradiction established by this passage. Do not identify the visitors as three named kings. [Matthew 2:9–16](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/2?id=p9-p16&lang=eng#p9).
- **Return to Nazareth:** The record gives the sequence following Egypt and Herod's death but no exact age for the carried child. Current metadata leaves that interval unstated. [Matthew 2:19–23](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/matt/2?id=p19-p23&lang=eng#p19).

## The two additional encounters

- **Paul in Athens:** Acts records Paul speaking and varied responses, including named believers, but supplies no portrait of Paul or the other listeners. The current caption does not identify any pictured listener as Damaris or Dionysius, and the image should not be called Paul's verified likeness. An Athenian architectural setting is contextually appropriate; the camera position, audience arrangement and precise backdrop are unverified. The altar Paul recalls passing need not be in the scene. [Acts 17:19–34](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/acts/17?id=p19-p34&lang=eng#p19).
- **Jesus and Mary near Bethany:** This is Mary, Lazarus's sister, before Lazarus is raised and before Christ's Resurrection. Her approach, falling at His feet, the companions' tears and Jesus's tears are recorded. His exact touch and their facial appearances are artistic interpretation. Current caption/source preserve these distinctions; no post-Resurrection wounds or invented appearance of a living Lazarus are required. [John 11:30–36](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/scriptures/nt/john/11?id=p30-p36&lang=eng#p30).

## Atmosphere and other scene boundaries

The reviewed images use stone/plaster, woven garments, wood, lamps, and landscape as plausible settings. This is not verification of exact first-century tailoring, temple furnishings, building geometry or particular household objects. The same limit applies to Isaiah's conduit encounter and the Book of Mormon settings: scripture grounds narrated actions, not an authenticated archaeological city model. The source research already distinguishes Nephi's vision, Joseph's dream, Samuel's prediction and the later sign's fulfillment. Current captions do not collapse those events into one time or place.

Direct image spot-checks in this pass: current 07 Zacharias, 09 Visitation, 16 Anna, 17 wise men, and 21 naming John; the Paul/Bethany images were also viewed during their integration. The remaining 18 Birth selections were checked through current metadata and the existing per-image technical review, not newly inspected at full resolution in this pass. Visible older ages fit the stated categories; no exact-age caption claim was found. There is no historical basis here to change the protected Christ portrait.
