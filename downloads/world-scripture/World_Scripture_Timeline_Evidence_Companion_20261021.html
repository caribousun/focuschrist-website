World Scripture Timeline — The Evidence

Every entry behind the timeline, with its dates, its location, its map coordinates, and the basis for each one, gathered in one document you can search and link through. Nothing here argues a conclusion. The data is presented as it stands, with its sources, so the reader can weigh it.

On the timeline itself, the People selector above the map isolates any one of these peoples, on the map and in the moment list, including while the years play. This companion is the same data in document form.

How the dates are set

World entries carry the dates given by the published sources named in each row's Basis column; where the published record genuinely disagrees, the entry's notes in the timeline say so rather than picking a winner silently. Bible entries after Abraham use commonly cited scholarly dates; the earliest follow the traditional chronology already in the owner's sheet.

Book of Mormon entries are dated where the record itself states the year, or one step of arithmetic from a count the record states: years from Lehi's departure in 600 BC, the year N of the reign of the judges as 92 − N BC, years from the sign of Christ's birth, and Mormon's own stated ages and years. Anything needing a chain of inferences is not plotted; the candidates that failed this rule are listed at the end of this document with their reasons. One bounded exception stands apart from that rule, and it is labeled wherever it appears: Ether states no absolute year and no total span, so the Jaredite entries (the tower departure, Ether's record of the Jaredite history, the last battle, and Coriantumr among the Mulekites) carry approximate years from traditional and commonly published placements, not from the text. The Notes column gives the basis and the uncertainty for each of them.

Book of Mormon land entries are model placements: each carries two coordinate pairs, one for the Mesoamerica setting and one for the North America setting, and both are listed. Entries tied to anchored places (Jerusalem, Arabia, the New York hill) carry a single pair.

Book of Mormon candidates that fail the dating rule

  • The great battle of Alma 28 — (tens of thousands slain after the sons of Mosiah return). No year statement anywhere in Alma 28. It can be placed only by sequence, between the sons' return (Alma 27) and the 17th-year frame of Alma 30:5. Sequence placement is more than one step
  • Moroni retakes Gid and frees the prisoners without bloodshed — (Alma 55). No year is stated in the chapter. It is bounded only by Alma 54:1 (year 29 frame for the epistles) and Alma 56:1 / 59:1 (year 30), which does not fix a single year
  • Alma's preaching tour of Zarahemla, Gideon, Melek, and Ammonihah — (Alma 5-8). Sequence filler inside the year-9 frame; the seat-yielding that opens the frame is already captured, and the tour itself has no year statement of its own
  • Zeniff-record internal events — (Mosiah 9-10 and onward: the first battle in the thirteenth year of Zeniff's reign, Mosiah 9:14; the twenty-two years of peace, Mosiah 10:3; king Laman's death and his son's war, Mosiah 10:6; and the later events down through Noah and Limhi). Every count in Zeniff's record is relative to Zeniff's return and reign, and the text never ties that anchor to a departure count in one step. The captured "Zeniff reclaim land south" anchor is itself approximate, so every chained date needs two or more steps
  • Ammon's expedition to the land of Lehi-Nephi — (Mosiah 7:1). Dated only as coming "after three years" of peace and inquiry, a relative marker, not a stated count from any anchor
  • Sherem's preaching and death — (Jacob 7). Jacob states no year count for the episode; Jacob 7:27 closes the chapter unnumbered
  • Chemish and Abinadom receive the plates — (Omni 1:8-11). No counts are stated for either transfer
  • King Benjamin's war with the Lamanites — (Words of Mormon 1:12-18). No year is stated
  • Jarom's 200-year mark — (Jarom 1:5). A count-mark status summary (the people waxed strong and kept the law of Moses), not a discrete event
  • Enos's wars and record-preservation prayers — (Enos 1:20-24). No years are stated
  • Lehi's death and other 1-2 Nephi events after the split — (for example 2 Nephi 4). No counts are given after the departure and split frames already captured
  • Helaman 11:21-23 peace and prosperity notices — (years 77-79) and similar year-frame status notices. States of affairs inside dated frames, not discrete events
  • Mormon's refusal to lead after the people's boasting — (Mormon 3:9-16). The frame straddles the stated 362 victory (Mormon 3:7-8) and Mormon 4:1 (363); no single year is stated for the refusal itself
  • War begins in Mormon's youth; Mormon carried to Zarahemla at about age eleven — (Mormon 1:6-11).   • Battles and movements of Mormon 2:9-16 — (the gathering, Jashon, the land of Shim) between the stated years 326 and the stand at Shem. Sequence only; no year statements
  • The Three Nephites' transformation and the second appearance to the disciples — (3 Nephi 27-28). Dated only as "after he ascended"; no year is stated
  • Gadianton combinations built up again — (4 Nephi 1:42). Narrated after the 260-year mark (1:41) with no year of its own
  • Zarahemla rebuilt — (4 Nephi 1:7-8). No year is attached; it is narrated inside the flow covering the years up to 59
  • The first generation from Christ passes away — (4 Nephi 1:18), and the parallel statement that the chosen disciples had all passed, save the three (4 Nephi 1:14). The text gives only a terminus (by the time 110 years, and 100 years respectively, had passed), not a dated occurrence
