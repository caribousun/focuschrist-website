Jesus Christ as Son of God, Savior and Redeemer, His Atonement, Resurrection, premortal role, and promised return.

What Is the Book of Mormon?

What the Book of Mormon claims to be, why it is called another testament of Jesus Christ, and why it does not replace the Bible.

Why Do Latter-day Saints Build Temples?

Sacred worship, covenants, ordinances, eternal families, and service for those who have died.

What Happens After Death?

The spirit world, resurrection through Jesus Christ, judgment, and the hope of eternal life.

Who Was Joseph Smith?

The First Vision, Book of Mormon, Restoration, prophetic role, and a responsible approach to his complex history.

How Do Prayer and Personal Revelation Work?

Prayer, the Holy Ghost, personal guidance, discernment, and what it can mean when an answer does not come immediately.

Why Are Families Important?

Divine identity, marriage and parenting, temple sealing, imperfect family realities, and eternal hope.

Temple sealing, Christ-centered equal partnership, covenant faithfulness, tender questions, eight scriptures, and a guided seven-day study.

From listening to living

One message. One meaningful step.

Use this optional practice on your own, with family, or in a class. Give yourself time to understand the message in its setting.

01 · Listen and read

Notice the witness of Christ

Read the full message or watch it on the official page. What does it teach about the Savior’s character, promises, or work?

02 · Follow the scriptures

Study beyond a sentence

Open the cited verses and read their surrounding passages. Write down one question and one connection you want to explore.

03 · Choose an invitation

Make room for action

In your own notebook, finish: “This week, I will…” Choose something specific, then return to the message and reflect on what you learned.

Choose a connected course of study

An answer can open a larger question. These short sequences let you follow that question without needing to read every article. Begin anywhere; the next page explains the subject in its own setting.

Christian identity, then the Savior’s mission

Start with the different meanings of “Christian.” Continue to the study of Jesus Christ, then to the Bible and Book of Mormon for the question of scripture and authority.

Read, compare, and reflect

Begin with the purpose of the Book of Mormon. Continue into a comparison with the Bible and then the prayer study, keeping textual understanding and spiritual questions distinct.

Resurrection, temples, and family hopes

Begin with the scriptural teaching about life after death. Continue to temple service and family relationships. If you are grieving, you may prefer the gentler child-loss or trials study first.

For each passage, try three questions: Who is speaking, and to whom? What does the passage actually say? What am I bringing to it from my own experience? This is an optional reading practice, not a test of belief. The Ask page offers another place to explore a question, and the linked sources let you check the response for yourself.

Questions and discipleshipChrist Walks Beside the Seeker

This scene reflects the Christian hope that sincere questions can become part of a continuing journey toward the Savior. He invites people to learn of Him, follow Him, and trust Him one step at a time.

The shared path expresses patient discipleship. Answers may come through scripture, prayer, the Holy Ghost, wise counsel, and continued faith as a seeker chooses to keep walking with Jesus Christ.

Come unto ChristBring Every Honest Question

Jesus Christ stands at the center of every lasting gospel answer. Questions about identity, purpose, repentance, suffering, hope, and eternal life find their fullest meaning in His teachings, Atonement, and Resurrection.

The warm light and steady expression invite quiet reflection on the living Savior. He knows each person perfectly and calls every seeker to come unto Him with faith, humility, and an honest heart.

Continue the journeyFaith Is a Path We Keep Walking

Discipleship is an ongoing path of learning, repentance, covenant, service, and trust in Jesus Christ. One answer can bring needed light while also opening the way to deeper study and faithful action.

The sunlit road and shared movement express the Savior's patient companionship. He does not ask disciples to understand the entire journey at once, but to receive His light and take the next faithful step.

Children are precious to HimThe Savior Welcomes Each One

During His ministry, Jesus welcomed children, took them in His arms, and blessed them. He taught His disciples not to turn them away.

Christ's open hand and attentive expression reflect a Savior who sees each child personally. His example calls disciples to receive, protect, teach, and care for children with patience and love.
