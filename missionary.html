Restoration and early missionaries

From a Book of Mormon in hand to a worldwide witness

Missionary work began before the Church was formally organized and gathered momentum during Joseph Smith's lifetime.

  1. 1829–1830

    The message begins to travel

    Early believers shared the Book of Mormon and testified of the Restoration even before the Church's organization.

  2. June 1830

    Samuel Smith is called

    Joseph Smith's brother Samuel became the first missionary called after the Church was organized, carrying copies of the Book of Mormon through communities near Palmyra, New York.

  3. 1830s–1840s

    Missions cross borders and oceans

    Missionaries traveled through the United States and Canada and then to England and the Pacific, often serving at great personal sacrifice.

    Local disciples build a global Church

    Missionaries serve among many cultures and languages while local members and leaders nurture faith in their own communities.

The missionary purpose

Help people draw closer to the Savior

Missionaries do more than deliver a message. They help people build faith in Jesus Christ, turn toward Him, prepare for baptism, receive the gift of the Holy Ghost, and continue along the covenant path.

That work is personal. It happens through prayer, scripture study, sincere conversation, patient listening, invitations to act in faith, and continued friendship.

A pattern for every disciple

Love, Share, Invite

Sharing the gospel is not limited to full-time missionaries. It can grow naturally from Christlike love, honest friendship, and respect for each person's agency.

Love

See the person first

Pray for people, listen without an agenda, and serve because they are children of God, not because you expect a particular response.

Share

Speak naturally of goodness

Share what gives you hope in Jesus Christ through ordinary conversation, personal experience, scripture, and quiet example.

Invite

Open a door without pressure

Offer a simple invitation to learn, worship, serve, or meet missionaries, then honor the answer with warmth and friendship.

Study before a conversation, or continue afterward

Meeting missionaries can bring both curiosity and uncertainty. You can prepare by reading a short passage and bringing your own questions. You may ask what a term means, request the source of a teaching, or say that you need more time. A respectful conversation makes room for your background and your choices.

In Matthew 28:18-20, the risen Jesus commissions His disciples to teach and baptize, and promises His presence. Read the commission with its focus on Him. It explains the Christian invitation to discipleship; it does not remove another person's freedom to consider, ask, or decline an invitation.

Start with Jesus Christ

Learn what Latter-day Saints mean by His Atonement and Resurrection. Bring a question about how those beliefs shape daily discipleship.

Read a passage before discussing the book

Explore its purpose and try a focused reading. Separate questions about its teachings from questions about its origin.

Consider the invitation to pray

Study prayer and discernment without assuming everyone receives an answer in the same way or at the same speed.

After a conversation, return to the original passage and write down what was explained, what you understood differently, and what remains open. You do not need to resolve every question in one visit.

Christ's commission The Savior Sends His Apostles

Matthew 28 records the risen Lord sending His disciples to teach and baptize among all nations. The commission joins proclamation with discipleship as people are invited to learn and live everything Christ taught.

Christ and the Twelve walking with purpose express the movement of that commission. The Apostles would carry His witness outward, establish the Church, minister in His name, and teach others to become disciples of the risen Lord.

Restoration beginnings Samuel Smith Carries the Message

Samuel Smith, Joseph Smith's brother, became the first missionary called after the Church was organized. In June 1830, he traveled through nearby communities carrying copies of the Book of Mormon and sharing the message of the Restoration.

The books in Samuel's arms and the long road ahead reflect the simple means and determined faith of early missionary service. His efforts seemed small at first, yet the message he carried helped prepare people to hear and share the restored gospel.

Across borders and oceans A Witness Carried to New Nations

During the 1830s and 1840s, missionaries traveled beyond their familiar communities into Canada, England, and the Pacific. Their service required long journeys, new languages, and faith amid uncertainty.

In 1837, Heber C. Kimball and six other missionaries opened the British Mission and began their labors in Preston. The harbor and ship recall the faith required to leave home, cross the Atlantic, and preach Jesus Christ in an unfamiliar land.

Learn the history How Early Missionary Work Began

Believers shared the Book of Mormon and testified of the Restoration even before the Church was formally organized. After April 1830, missionary calls gave that witness an expanding pattern and purpose.

The official Church History topic follows these early missionaries, the messages they carried, and the communities their service reached.

From local journeys to global service How the Work Expanded

Missionary work grew from short journeys near the Church's beginnings into service across borders, oceans, cultures, and languages. Local members and leaders then helped faith take root in their own communities.

The official history traces that growth while keeping the focus on individual disciples, personal sacrifice, and testimony of Jesus Christ.

Centered on Jesus Christ One Savior, One Invitation

The missionary purpose is to invite others to come unto Christ by helping them receive the restored gospel. Teaching, baptism, the gift of the Holy Ghost, and continued discipleship all point toward a deeper relationship with Him.

Placing Christ at the center expresses the purpose shared by every faithful assignment. Whether missionaries teach, serve, support congregations, or strengthen communities, their work is meant to help people feel His love and draw nearer to Him.

Every nation and people A Global Work Lived Locally

Missionaries serve among many cultures and languages, while local members and leaders continue building faith and belonging in their own communities. The worldwide Church grows through one relationship and one invitation at a time.

The connected communities and distant horizons reflect one gospel carried into many circumstances. The work becomes personal as missionaries learn a people's language, listen to their questions, serve beside them, and testify of Jesus Christ.

Listen, teach, and invite Begin With the Person

Missionary teaching begins with listening. Missionaries seek to understand a person's questions, beliefs, and hopes, then teach the gospel of Jesus Christ clearly and invite personal action.

The open scriptures, attentive expressions, and seated circle emphasize teaching with people rather than merely speaking to them. Meaningful missionary lessons make room for questions, the Holy Ghost, and each person's freedom to choose.

Love expressed through service Different Gifts, the Same Savior

Service missionaries use their abilities to help meet local needs through assignments suited to their circumstances. Young adults, senior couples, and individuals may serve in different ways while sharing a Christ-centered purpose.

The shared work at the table reflects discipleship expressed through willing hands. Service missionaries help care for practical needs while following the Savior's example of noticing individuals, lifting burdens, and serving with compassion.
