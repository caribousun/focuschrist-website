The Good Shepherd

Inspirational Art / The Good Shepherd

Jesus used the image of a shepherd to describe His relationship with those who follow Him: He knows His sheep, calls them, leads them, protects them, and gives His life for them.

Study in depthResourcesContinue this subject

“I Am the Good Shepherd”

In John 10, Christ contrasts a true shepherd with a hired servant who abandons the flock when danger comes. The Savior’s claim reaches its deepest meaning in His willingness to lay down His life and take it up again.

Known and Led

The shepherd image also expresses personal care. Christian discipleship is not merely following an abstract moral system. It is learning to recognize the voice of Jesus Christ through scripture, prayer, covenant, and the influence of the Holy Ghost, then choosing to follow where He leads.

Read the shepherd image in context

Begin with John 10:1-18. Jesus develops an image of a flock, a gate, and a shepherd before identifying Himself as the good shepherd. The contrast with a hired hand matters: His care is shown in His willingness to remain with the sheep and give His life for them. Verses 17-18 emphasize that He lays down His life willingly and has power to take it again.

Being known is more than being counted

Read John 10:11-16, 27-30 slowly. Notice the relationship expressed through knowing, hearing, and following. Within this account, the shepherd's voice belongs to Jesus. Applying the image to discipleship should keep His character and teaching at the center; it should not make unquestioning obedience to another person a substitute for following Christ.

Read Psalm 23 alongside John 10

Psalm 23 moves from pasture and still water into a valley overshadowed by death and a table in the presence of enemies. The psalm's comfort includes God's presence in danger. It is not a promise that faithful people will never suffer, need protection, or ask for help.

Compare the two passages without collapsing their differences. Psalm 23 voices trust in the Lord as shepherd; John 10 presents Jesus's claim about His own identity and self-giving care. Which words describe provision? Which describe presence? Which describe what Christ does for His sheep?

Look, reflect, and choose a small response

The artwork gives visual form to a scriptural image. It does not document the precise appearance of Jesus or prove what a particular spiritual impression means. As an optional reflection, consider one way to care for someone who feels overlooked: listen, make a practical offer, or keep a commitment. Let the person decide what help would be welcome.

If recognizing divine guidance is your question, continue into the prayer study for scripture, thoughtful discernment, and room for uncertainty. If the valley language feels close to your own life, the study of faith during trials explores both spiritual hope and receiving human support.
