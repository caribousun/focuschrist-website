A path through this study

Listen for the Shepherd's Voice

Look first at the Savior's face and the life moving around Him. He is not distant from the street or the people in it. John 10 gives language to that closeness: He knows, calls, leads, protects, and lays down His life for the sheep.

Look

Notice the Savior's expression and the people nearest Him in the crowded street.

Read

Listen for every action Jesus uses to describe the Good Shepherd in John 10.

Respond

Consider one person you can notice, call, or walk beside with greater care.

Scripture study

Known, Led, and Loved

In John 10:1-18, Jesus describes a relationship built on recognition and trust. The shepherd knows the sheep and calls them; the sheep recognize his voice and follow. The image grows deeper when Jesus says the Good Shepherd gives His life for the sheep. His care is not sentimental. It is costly, faithful, and personal.

Psalm 23 adds the language of provision, rest, guidance, and companionship through the valley of the shadow of death. The shepherd does not promise a path with no valley. He promises His presence through it.

The sheep beyond the present fold

Jesus speaks of “other sheep” in John 10:16. In 3 Nephi 15:11-24, the risen Christ identifies the people at Bountiful with that promise. Read the two passages together and notice how His voice reaches people in more than one place.

Quiet reflection

Hear and Follow

Choose the prompt that feels most honest today.

Where have I noticed the Shepherd's care?

Read Psalm 23 slowly. Name one place where you have received rest, direction, nourishment, or companionship. Offer thanks for the person or moment through which that care came.

Which voice am I learning to trust?

John 10 describes sheep recognizing the shepherd's voice. Compare a thought that leads toward Christlike love with one that produces contempt or despair. What fruit does each voice bear?

Who might need someone to walk beside them?

Think of one person who may feel unseen or burdened. Choose a small act of steady care: listen, send a message, share a meal, or help with something practical.

Study the Shepherd's Compassion

In Our Good Shepherd, Elder Dale G. Renlund teaches about the Savior's compassion for imperfect people and His joy as they heal. You can also follow the theme through the Good Shepherd entry in the Guide to the Scriptures.
