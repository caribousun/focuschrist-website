Finding Hope After the Death of a Child

Answers / Death of a child

The death of a child leaves questions that a short answer cannot settle. This page offers a place to begin with the Savior's compassion, the scriptural promise concerning little children, and room to mourn without having to explain away the loss.

This independent study draws on Latter-day Saint scripture. It does not claim to know why a particular child died or prescribe how a family should grieve.

What Scripture Says About Little Children

Moroni 8:8-12, 19 teaches that little children are alive in Christ through His mercy and do not need baptism or repentance. Their salvation is not lost because they died without baptism.

Doctrine and Covenants 137:10 teaches that children who die before the age of accountability are saved in the celestial kingdom. This is a specific promise about children before accountability. It should not be stretched into claims about every circumstance surrounding a death.

When the Child You Lost Was Older

A parent's love does not end at a particular birthday. When the child who died was a teenager or adult, the specific teaching about little children should not be applied beyond its stated scope. Doctrine and Covenants 137:7-9 also teaches that God considers people's works and the desires of their hearts. Leave judgments about another person's eternal standing with Him.

Christ's Compassion Is a Place to Begin

Alma 7:11-13 describes the Savior bearing pains, sicknesses, and infirmities so He would know how to help His people. This teaching offers a foundation for turning toward Him with sorrow as well as trust.

These promises need not become a demand to feel better immediately. You can bring a prayer of gratitude, a question, or simply the words that today is hard. This page offers no timetable for grief and no explanation that God needed your child more than you did.

For Someone Supporting the Family

Consider asking what would help this week: a meal, an errand, quiet company, or space. Let the family lead when speaking about their child. Avoid making the death into a lesson they are expected to accept. Offer help you can follow through on, and ask before sharing their story with others.

Questions for Quiet Reflection

What do I need today?

Would it help to be heard, to rest, to remember my child, or to ask someone for a specific kind of support? I do not need to resolve every question today.

Which promise can I return to?

Read one of the passages above slowly. What does it actually teach, and what questions does it leave unanswered? Allow both to be part of honest prayer.
