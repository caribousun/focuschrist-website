The Abrahamic Covenant

God’s promise to Abraham reaches toward every family. Follow its story through scripture, and discover ways to live it with Christ today.

A promise to live by

God meets people through covenants across scripture: His promise after the Flood, Israel’s calling at Sinai, and the commitments of baptism. These have distinct settings, but each invites us to consider His faithfulness and our response. Genesis 9:8-17 · Exodus 19:5-6 · Mosiah 18:8-10

A covenant with God is a sacred commitment. At baptism, we promise to serve Him and keep His commandments. Alma makes that personal: carry burdens, comfort someone who mourns, and stand as a witness of God. Mosiah 18:8-10

The Abrahamic covenant draws us toward Jesus Christ. Paul teaches that those who belong to Christ become Abraham’s heirs. This study follows that promise through the Bible, Book of Mormon, Doctrine and Covenants, and Pearl of Great Price. Galatians 3:26-29

Abraham and Sarah learn to trust

Abraham and Sarah travel toward Canaan, dwelling in tents along the way. In Abraham’s account, the journey follows the Lord’s call at Haran. Their life of trust unfolds through travel, waiting, and the responsibilities of a household. Abraham 2:6–16

God names Sarah in His promise. Genesis later tells of Isaac’s birth and her laughter in old age. We can rejoice with her while gently remembering that faithful people experience waiting and parenthood differently. Genesis 17:15-21 · Genesis 21:1-7

The family story also includes Hagar and Ishmael’s painful departure. In the wilderness, God hears the boy and helps Hagar find water. As we read, their distress invites our compassion alongside Sarah’s joy. Genesis 21:13-20

A blessing for every family

At Haran, Jehovah, understood by Latter-day Saints as the premortal Jesus Christ, promises Abraham that his family will carry the gospel and priesthood to all nations. These blessings reach every family: salvation and eternal life. Those who receive the gospel are counted among Abraham’s seed. Abraham 2:6–12 · Abraham 2:9–11

In a later vision, the Lord speaks face to face with Abraham and places His hand upon Abraham’s eyes. Abraham sees the Lord’s creations, and the promise of descendants follows. This intimate account invites wonder at the One who knows and blesses him. Abraham 3:11–15

Paul centers the promise in Christ: those who belong to Him become Abraham’s heirs. This invitation reaches beyond ancestry and calls us to bless others, never to claim superiority. Galatians 3:26–29 · Genesis 17:7–8

Faithful through the generations

During a famine, the Lord appears to Isaac and tells him to remain in the land He directs. He promises His presence and renews the oath made to Abraham: through Isaac’s descendants, all nations will be blessed. The promise continues into another generation. Genesis 26:2–5

God’s faithfulness also shapes how we treat others. Paul says He has not cast away His people and warns against boasting over Israel. Nephi connects gathering with God’s covenants. We can welcome people toward Christ while honoring Jewish people and refusing prejudice. Romans 11:1–2 · Romans 11:17–29 · 1 Nephi 22:8–12

Jacob’s dream at Bethel

Isaac sends Jacob on his journey with the blessing of Abraham. When night comes, Jacob lies down with a stone for his pillow. In a dream, he sees a ladder reaching heaven, with angels ascending and descending. Genesis 28:3–4 · Genesis 28:10–12

The Lord identifies Himself as the God of Abraham and Isaac. He promises Jacob descendants, land, and a blessing reaching every family. He also promises to stay with Jacob and bring him home. Jacob wakes with reverence for the place where God has met him. Genesis 28:13–17

Later, Jesus tells Nathanael that he will see heaven open and angels ascending and descending upon the Son of Man. Read beside Jacob’s dream, this shared imagery invites us to ponder how Jesus Christ brings us near to God. John 1:50–51

Jacob receives the name Israel

Before meeting Esau again, Jacob prays for protection and remembers God’s promise. That night, alone near the Jabbok, he wrestles until dawn and asks for a blessing. He receives the name Israel, then leaves with a limp as the sun rises. Genesis 32:9–12 · Genesis 32:22–31

Genesis calls the wrestler a man, while Jacob says he has seen God face to face. Hosea speaks of an angel and remembers Jacob’s tears and prayer. These passages invite reverent attention without giving us a certain portrait of the one who met him. Genesis 32:24–30 · Hosea 12:3–6

Later at Bethel, God again names Jacob Israel and renews the promises given to Abraham and Isaac. The family story moves forward through struggle, worship, and God’s faithfulness. We can bring our own fears to Him and keep seeking His blessing. Genesis 35:9–15

Jesus Christ at the heart of the promise

Jesus taught that Abraham rejoiced to see His day. He spoke of His existence before Abraham, directing His hearers beyond ancestry to His own divine identity. The promise finds its saving center in Him. John 8:56-58 · Galatians 3:14-16

In another setting, Jesus saw a woman who had been unable to stand upright for eighteen years. He healed her and called her a daughter of Abraham. As she praised God, covenant belonging became deeply personal: He saw her suffering and acted with mercy. Luke 13:10-17

At Solomon’s porch in winter, Jesus speaks of those who hear His voice and follow Him. He promises them eternal life. In this later conversation, we hear the familiar invitation to trust the Lord, listen, and follow. John 10:22–30

The risen Lord speaks of the covenant

Before speaking of Abraham’s covenant, the risen Jesus gives bread to His disciples to share with the standing multitude. He also gives wine, and the people are filled with the Spirit. The meal draws their attention to the Savior who nourishes them. 3 Nephi 20:1–9

The risen Savior gives bread to His disciples to share. Explore this earlier moment in the same chapter.

3 Nephi 20:1–9

Open study

Among the people at Bountiful, the risen Savior spoke of the covenant made with Abraham. He described His blessing in terms of turning people from sin. The promise reaches into a changed life, not simply a remembered ancestry. 3 Nephi 20:25-27

He also invited Gentiles to repent, come to Him, and be numbered among His people. The Father’s gathering prepares people to come to Christ. We take part through humble witness, welcome, and a willingness to turn toward Him ourselves. 3 Nephi 21:22-29

Promises carried into our day

Doctrine and Covenants 110 records successive visions in the Kirtland Temple. Moses brought keys of gathering; Elias committed the gospel of Abraham; Elijah announced the turning of hearts between generations. Their messages are distinct and belong together in the Restoration’s covenant story. Doctrine and Covenants 110:11-16

Priesthood ordinances point us toward knowing God. Baptism gives daily shape to that relationship as we serve, keep His commandments, and comfort one another. A covenant can be remembered in an ordinary act of care. Doctrine and Covenants 84:19-22 · Mosiah 18:8-10

Turning hearts can begin with listening to an elder or learning an ancestor’s name. Such moments can deepen our love for the people we remember in temple service. Elijah’s message reaches into both sacred ordinances and the care we give one another. Doctrine and Covenants 110:13-16 · About the Temple Endowment

Nearer to Him, bound to one another

Doctrine and Covenants 132 describes Abraham’s promises continuing beyond this life. Eternal life centers on knowing God and Jesus Christ. Temple worship invites us toward that relationship as we prepare prayerfully, keep our covenants, and learn to follow the Savior. Doctrine and Covenants 132:19–24 · Doctrine and Covenants 132:29–32 · About the Temple Endowment

The Church describes the endowment as a gift that teaches God’s plan and strengthens commitment to Jesus Christ. Its covenants include obedience, sacrifice, gospel living, chastity, and consecration. Temple sealing joins husband and wife, and families across generations, through covenants honored in faithfulness. About the Temple Endowment · About Temple Sealings · Doctrine and Covenants 132:19-24

If you are unmarried, childless, grieving, or carrying a painful family story, you belong here now. The Church teaches hope for blessings beyond the opportunities of this life. While their timing remains unknown, we can receive Christ’s love and share it today. About Temple Sealings · Galatians 3:26-29

Other prophetic visions widen this hope. Enoch hears of Zion’s people meeting in a joyful embrace and dwelling with the Lord. John sees a new heaven and earth, with God among His people and death and sorrow ended. Their distinct visions invite hope in God’s lasting presence. Moses 7:63–64 · Revelation 21:1–4
