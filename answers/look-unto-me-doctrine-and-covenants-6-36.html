Look Unto Me in Every Thought

“Look unto me in every thought; doubt not, fear not.” Doctrine and Covenants 6:36

Answers / Doctrine and Covenants 6:36

The Lord's words to Joseph Smith and Oliver Cowdery do more than tell disciples not to be afraid. They direct attention to Jesus Christ Himself. Read in the setting of Doctrine and Covenants 6:32-37, the invitation moves from Christ's presence, to doing good, to building on His rock, to looking to Him, and finally to beholding the wounds of the risen Savior.

The Setting: Oliver Cowdery Comes to Harmony

Oliver Cowdery arrived in Harmony, Pennsylvania, on April 5, 1829, and began serving as Joseph Smith's scribe for the Book of Mormon translation on April 7. Doctrine and Covenants 6 was received later that month. The revelation gave Oliver counsel and confirmation about his part in the Lord's work. It also spoke to concerns he had carried privately, reminding him of peace the Lord had already spoken to his mind.

This setting matters. “Doubt not, fear not” was given in the middle of real work, responsibility, uncertainty, and opposition. The Lord did not merely tell Oliver to manufacture confidence. He taught him where to direct his attention.

Read Doctrine and Covenants 6:32-37 as One Invitation

“There will I be in the midst of them”

Verse 32 begins with presence. Christ tells His servants that when they gather in His name, He is in their midst. The foundation for courage is therefore not self-confidence alone, but the Savior's promised presence.

“Fear not to do good”

Verses 33-34 turn attention toward faithful action. Disciples can sow good even when opposition is real. The Lord's image of being built upon His rock places stability in Him rather than in favorable circumstances.

“Look unto me in every thought”

Verse 36 gives the central invitation. Looking to Christ can include remembering His teachings, measuring choices against His example, turning to Him in prayer, and returning attention to Him when fear begins to dominate thought. It does not require pretending that difficult questions have disappeared.

“Behold the wounds”

Verse 37 makes the invitation unmistakably Christ-centered. Immediately after “doubt not, fear not,” the Lord directs Joseph and Oliver to His wounded side, hands, and feet. The passage points beyond positive thinking to the crucified and risen Jesus Christ. His identity, sacrifice, Resurrection, commandments, and promises are the reason the invitation to trust Him has substance.

What Does “In Every Thought” Mean?

The verse can be read as an invitation to orient a life toward Christ, not as a demand to experience a particular religious thought every second. Scripture study, prayer, repentance, worship, service, and deliberate remembrance can repeatedly turn the mind back toward Him. When a decision is unclear, a useful question is not only “What outcome do I want?” but also “What choice helps me follow Jesus Christ faithfully with what I know now?”

What If I Do Not Know What God Wants Me to Do?

Doctrine and Covenants 6 does not remove the need to think, choose, and act. Oliver's later experiences in Doctrine and Covenants 8 and 9 show that seeking revelation can involve desire, study, effort, correction, and patience. When no detailed instruction is apparent, you can continue praying and studying while making responsible choices consistent with what God has already revealed. Uncertainty need not become spiritual paralysis.

What If Prayer Seems Unanswered?

Earlier in the same revelation, the Lord reminds Oliver of a previous experience: “Did I not speak peace to your mind concerning the matter?” (Doctrine and Covenants 6:23). Sometimes the next faithful step is to remember what has already been received rather than demand a new manifestation. At other times, a person may still need to wait, gather information, seek wise counsel, and continue faithfully without claiming an answer that has not come.

How Can Looking to Christ Change Fear?

The passage does not say that disciples will never feel fear. It places fear beside a larger reality: Christ is present, good is still worth doing, His rock remains secure, and His wounds testify of what He has done. Looking to Him can change what governs the next decision even before circumstances change.

A Simple Study Practice

Read Doctrine and Covenants 6:32-37 slowly. On a page, write four headings: What Christ says about Himself, What Christ asks me to do, What I am afraid of, and One faithful next step. Keep the final column small and concrete. The purpose is not to force an impression. It is to place the concern deliberately before Christ and act on what is already clear.

Questions for deeper reflection

What thought repeatedly pulls my attention away from Christ? What truth about Him in verses 32-37 answers that thought? Is fear preventing a good action that is already within my responsibility? What changes when I read verse 36 together with verse 37 rather than alone?

The Meaning of 636 at @theRisen636

The “636” in @theRisen636 points to Doctrine and Covenants 6:36 and its invitation to look to Jesus Christ in every thought. That same invitation fits the purpose of focusChrist: study should ultimately direct attention beyond the site and toward the Savior.
