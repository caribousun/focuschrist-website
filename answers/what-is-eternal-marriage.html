Covenant, Love, and Eternal Hope

Eternal Marriage

What Is Eternal Marriage?

A temple sealing points husband and wife toward Jesus Christ—and asks them to build an equal, faithful partnership with Him at the center.

Continue↓
Answers / Eternal marriage

Latter-day Saints believe a husband and wife may be sealed in a temple by priesthood authority so their marriage can continue after death. The ceremony is sacred, but eternal marriage is more than a single day: it is a covenant life of faith in Jesus Christ, repentance, loyalty, service, and love.

This teaching can touch deep hopes and deep wounds. Marital status does not measure a person’s worth or place in the Church. Covenants never require anyone to remain in danger or tolerate abuse.

Christ at the center

A sealing begins a covenant journey

Jesus taught that God intends marriage to be a union, not a contest for power. Latter-day Saint teaching adds that the sealing authority Christ described can bind on earth and in heaven. In the temple, spouses covenant with God and with one another. Those promises are made possible through Jesus Christ and become a daily invitation to follow His way.

Eternal does not mean that an unhealthy pattern should simply last forever. A Christ-centered marriage grows through agency, humility, equal counsel, forgiveness, fidelity, tenderness, and truth. Both spouses remain responsible for their own choices, and God’s judgment is perfectly just and merciful.

Marriage through time

One gospel ideal, lived in many circumstances

These symbolic scenes move from Eden to the present. They are invitations to study—not depictions of temple ordinances or claims about particular historical couples. In every scene, Jesus Christ is the Savior and Teacher who calls His disciples to covenant love, equal partnership, fidelity, and mercy.

01 · Eden

The beginning: companionship and shared stewardship

Latter-day Saints read Adam and Eve’s union as marriage established by God. Their story begins with companionship, cleaving to one another, and becoming one while each remains a moral agent.

Read Moses 3:18–24

02 · Ancient home

Covenant character grows in ordinary life

A lasting marriage is shaped in daily choices: loyalty, honest work, hospitality, patience, and care. The setting is symbolic; the principle is that sacred promises are lived at home.

Read Ecclesiastes 4:9–12

03 · The Savior’s world

Christ teaches unity without domination

Jesus pointed marriage back to God’s intention that husband and wife become one. His gospel also rejects control and invites both disciples to lead through service, truth, repentance, and love.

Read Matthew 19:4–6

04 · Restoration

Sealing hope returns

Latter-day Saints believe Elijah restored sealing keys in the Kirtland Temple. That authority joins family hope to Jesus Christ’s power over death and asks covenant keepers to live faithfully now.

Read Doctrine and Covenants 110:13–16

05 · Dignity and belonging

Divine worth is never a marital ranking

A temple sealing is sacred in Latter-day Saint belief, but it is not a measure of human worth. People who are civilly married, single, divorced, widowed, in an interfaith family, or not yet sealed deserve full dignity, welcome, and Christlike care.

Study belonging in the Church

06 · Today and onward

A covenant journey, not a perfect photograph

Temple sealing begins a life of discipleship. Husband and wife walk side by side as equal partners, relying on the Savior’s grace and choosing again and again to counsel, repent, forgive, protect, and serve.

Study equal partnership with Elder Soares
Every person is of infinite worth.Marriage and sealing status describe circumstances and covenants—not a hierarchy of souls. Jesus Christ’s invitation to come unto Him is extended to all.

Questions people carry

Clear answers with room for individual circumstances

Is a temple wedding automatically eternal?

No. The sealing ordinance opens eternal promises, but scripture connects those promises with covenant faithfulness and ratification by the Holy Spirit of Promise. A ceremony should not be treated as a guarantee that makes choices or agency irrelevant.

What is different from a civil marriage?

A civil marriage establishes a legal union. A temple sealing is a priesthood ordinance in which husband and wife make sacred covenants with God and each other, with promises that can extend beyond death. Legal requirements still matter, and local practice varies.

What if one spouse stops believing or breaks covenants?

Each person retains agency and answers to God for individual choices. A faithful person is not mechanically stripped of every promised blessing by another person’s decisions. God will not force an unwilling or unworthy relationship to continue eternally, and individual outcomes belong to His perfect judgment.

What about someone who is single, divorced, or widowed?

They fully belong now. Church teaching promises every blessing of exaltation to faithful covenant keepers, but it is wise not to speculate about when, how, or with whom an individual promise will be fulfilled. Present marital status is not a spiritual ranking.

Does forgiveness mean remaining in an unsafe marriage?

No. Covenant language never requires tolerating abuse. Forgiveness, legal decisions, personal safety, Church membership, and the future of a marriage are related but distinct questions. Seek immediate safety and qualified local help when harm or threats are present.

How can Christ be at the center today?

Pray and worship together when both are willing; listen without preparing a defense; repent for your own conduct; share decisions; keep confidences and promises; serve in small ways; and invite help early. One spouse cannot do the agency-work of the other.

A seven-day study pattern

Move from doctrine to discipleship

  1. AuthorityRead Matthew 16:19. Write what Christ’s sealing authority means to you.
  2. CovenantRead D&C 131:1–4 and 132:19 in context. Note every condition and promise.
  3. CharityRead Moroni 7:45–48. Choose one quality to practice without keeping score.
  4. Equal partnershipWatch Elder Soares. Ask where listening and shared counsel could deepen.
  5. RepentanceWatch Elder Clayton. Name one change that belongs to you, not your spouse.
  6. HopeRead Elder Carpenter. Bring an unresolved sealing question to God without forcing an answer.
  7. ChristRead John 17:20–23. Pray for unity founded in truth, love, and willing discipleship.
A reflection to keep

What does the Savior invite me to become in my closest relationships? Record one impression and one loving, safe action you can take. If you are studying alone, the same pattern can strengthen your preparation, healing, family relationships, and covenant discipleship.
