How Do Prayer and Personal Revelation Work?

Answers / Prayer and revelation

Latter-day Saints believe Heavenly Father hears sincere prayer and can give personal guidance through the Holy Ghost. They also believe spiritual impressions should be sought humbly, tested carefully, and kept within the responsibilities God has actually given a person.

Personal revelation is a religious belief and spiritual practice, not a substitute for professional, legal, medical, or emergency help when those are needed.

Prayer Is Communication with Heavenly Father

Latter-day Saint prayer is directed to God the Father in the name of Jesus Christ. Prayer can include gratitude, confession, worship, requests for help, questions, and quiet listening. The purpose is not to force a preferred answer from God but to turn toward Him with sincerity and willingness to follow Him.

What Is Personal Revelation?

Personal revelation means divine guidance given to an individual for that person’s life, needs, relationships, and responsibilities. Latter-day Saints believe the Holy Ghost can bring peace, warning, understanding, remembrance, conviction, or quiet impressions. Revelation may come gradually rather than as a dramatic event.

How Can Someone Prepare to Receive Guidance?

Official Church teaching emphasizes prayer, scripture study, repentance, obedience, worship, and patient effort. A person may study a question, make a thoughtful decision, ask God for confirmation or correction, and remain open to an answer that differs from the original preference.

How Can Spiritual Impressions Be Discerned?

Latter-day Saints are taught not to treat every feeling as revelation. Genuine guidance should be consistent with God’s revealed commandments and the teachings of Jesus Christ. It should not authorize a person to control someone else’s agency or claim revelation for responsibilities that belong to another. Time, scripture, wise counsel, repeated impressions, and the fruits produced by a decision can help with discernment.

What If No Answer Comes?

Silence does not necessarily mean rejection. Sometimes the invitation is to wait, keep living faithfully, gather more information, or exercise agency without a detailed instruction. Latter-day Saint teaching allows for both unmistakable spiritual direction and ordinary decisions made through faithful judgment.
