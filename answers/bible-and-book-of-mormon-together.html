How Do the Bible and Book of Mormon Work Together?

Answers / Scripture

Latter-day Saints read the Bible and Book of Mormon as distinct records that testify together of Jesus Christ. The point is not that one book makes the other unnecessary, but that multiple scriptural witnesses can deepen understanding of the same Savior and gospel.

Study in depthResourcesContinue this subject
This page focuses on how the two books function together in Latter-day Saint study. For a basic introduction to the Book of Mormon itself, see the related page below.

The Bible Records God's Dealings with His People

Latter-day Saints revere the Bible as the word of God and study both the Old and New Testaments. The Bible records God’s covenant dealings with Israel, the mortal ministry of Jesus Christ, His Crucifixion and Resurrection, and the teachings of His early Apostles.

The Book of Mormon Adds Another Historical Witness

The Book of Mormon describes prophets and peoples in the ancient Americas. It teaches of Christ before His birth and culminates in an account of the resurrected Savior ministering to people there. Latter-day Saints see this as another witness that Jesus is the Christ rather than an alternative version of Christianity.

They Reinforce Shared Doctrines

Readers encounter overlapping teachings about faith in Jesus Christ, repentance, baptism, the Holy Ghost, resurrection, judgment, charity, prayer, covenant, and discipleship. When one passage raises a question, a passage in the other book may provide additional context or a second witness.

They Also Preserve Different Voices and Settings

The books are not identical. Their authors lived in different lands and circumstances, and their records contain different histories. That difference is part of why Latter-day Saints value them together: agreement across separate scriptural records is understood as corroborating testimony.

A Practical Way to Study Them Together

A seeker can begin with a theme such as the Resurrection, grace, baptism, or the Sermon on the Mount, read relevant Bible passages, and then compare related Book of Mormon passages. The goal should be to understand Christ more clearly, not to force every passage into artificial agreement.

Read Two Short Passages Side by Side

Try Matthew 5:3-9 and 3 Nephi 12:3-9. Both passages include blessings for those who mourn, show mercy, and make peace. Notice the wording and setting of each account. Which teaching would you like to understand more fully or put into practice this week?

Compare Without Losing the Context

After reading a pair of passages, return to the surrounding chapter. Who is speaking, who is listening, and what question or situation is being addressed? Write down similarities and differences before deciding what they mean. A comparison is more useful when each author is allowed to speak in context.

For another starting point, read 1 Corinthians 15:3-8 alongside 3 Nephi 11:10-17. Paul recounts witnesses of the risen Christ; the Book of Mormon presents an account of people encountering Him. Latter-day Saints receive both as testimony of His Resurrection. Reading the accounts explains that belief without requiring a newcomer to have already accepted it.

A Guided Comparison: Grace and Discipleship

Begin with Ephesians 2:8-10 and 2 Nephi 31:19-21. Read the surrounding paragraphs rather than extracting a single phrase. Paul emphasizes salvation as a gift and speaks of good works; Nephi directs continuing disciples to rely on Christ while living with hope and love.

Keep two columns in a notebook: what this passage says, and what I infer from it. Ask what each writer emphasizes and what problem or invitation the passage addresses. The two accounts do not need to use identical vocabulary to be compared thoughtfully. Where their wording raises a question, preserve the question instead of forcing a quick agreement.

Let the next passage answer a real question

If you wonder what faith looks like in daily life, James 2:14-18 examines the response to a person without adequate food or clothing. As a reflection, consider whether your study is changing how you notice other people. That application grows from the passage's example; it is not a measure by which to judge someone else's private faith.

What if I accept the Bible but am unsure about the Book of Mormon?

You can compare the texts without pretending the question of authority is settled. Read each as it presents itself, identify shared and differing teachings, and then investigate the reasons Latter-day Saints accept the additional record. Respectful study leaves room for a reader to reach their own conclusion.
