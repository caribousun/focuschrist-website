A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Two Sons

One son changes his mind; another never turns agreement into action. Jesus makes the difference between a response and its appearance clear.

Authority, John, and response

Jesus tells this story in Jerusalem after religious leaders challenge His authority. The discussion has turned to John the Baptist and their response to him. A father asking two sons to work in his vineyard provides a direct way to examine the difference between words and obedience. Matthew 21:23-32

Matthew 21:23-32

Read and discover

What the sons actually do

The first son refuses, then changes his mind and goes. The second speaks agreeably but does not go. Jesus asks which did the father's will. The question is simple enough to answer, but its simplicity exposes the distance between a respectable response and an actual response.

Jesus applies the account to people who believed John and to hearers who, even after seeing that response, did not repent and believe. The story is therefore not merely praise for independent action. Its setting concerns receiving a call from God and allowing that call to change conduct.

Matthew 21:23-32

Read and discover

There is still room to change

There is hope in the first son's changed mind. An earlier refusal need not be the last word. You might consider one promise that remains only words, or one neglected good you can still begin. The passage invites a concrete response without requiring a polished account of your intentions.

Pause and consider

  • Where might a changed mind become a faithful action?
  • What promise still needs to become practice?
