A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Unjust Steward

A failing manager prepares urgently for his dismissal. This difficult parable becomes clearer when read with Jesus' teaching about honesty and faithfulness.

The steward faces an account

A rich man hears that his steward has wasted his goods and calls him to account. Facing removal, the steward considers how he will live afterward. The story begins with failing stewardship and an urgent attempt to prepare for a changed future. Luke 16:1-13

Luke 16:1-13

Read and discover

Preparation without approval of dishonesty

He calls his master's debtors and reduces their bills. One who owes oil is told to write a smaller amount, and another who owes wheat does the same. His purpose is to secure welcome in their houses when he loses his position.

The commendation concerns his shrewd action in view of what is coming. It does not turn the story into approval of dishonesty. Jesus' following words stress faithfulness in little and much, responsibility with what belongs to another, and the impossibility of serving both God and mammon.

Luke 16:1-13

Read the story with Jesus' conclusion

The surrounding teaching is essential because the narrative character is not a complete model for discipleship. The Bible Dictionary likewise cautions against treating every trait of a parable's character as part of the spiritual comparison. We can learn from foresight without approving the steward's conduct.

Reflection may ask how present choices prepare you for future responsibility. Resources can be used thoughtfully and generously while integrity remains nonnegotiable. The account's difficulty is a reason to read its conclusion carefully, not to excuse manipulation when it seems likely to produce a useful result.

Pause and consider

  • How are present choices preparing you for future responsibility?
  • Where does honesty need to govern your use of resources?
