A comparison taught by Jesus Christ

The Grain of Wheat

As His hour approaches, Jesus speaks of a grain that falls into the earth. The image connects His coming death with life and fruitfulness.

The hour approaches

Some Greeks have asked to see Jesus when He announces that the hour has come for the Son of Man to be glorified. He speaks of a grain of wheat falling into the earth and dying. The agricultural image belongs to the approach of His death, not to an unrelated lesson about farming. John 12:20-33

John 12:20-33

Read and discover

A grain and its fruit

A grain that remains alone is contrasted with one that, through the process described, bears much fruit. Jesus places this image beside words about losing and keeping one's life and about the servant following where He is.

The comparison prepares readers to understand that His approaching death is not simply the termination of His work. The passage joins sacrifice with fruitfulness while the surrounding chapter makes clear that He faces a real and troubling hour.

John 12:20-33

Read and discover

Following without denying the cost

A reflection on relinquishment should remain connected to following Christ in love and faithfulness. This is not an invitation to self-harm or a claim that every personal loss will produce a visible compensating benefit. You might instead ask what self-protective habit makes it harder to serve or follow honestly.

Pause and consider

  • How does this prepare readers for the Passion?
  • What might following Him require you to release?
