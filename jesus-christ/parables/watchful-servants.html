A parable taught by Jesus Christ

Servants Waiting for Their Lord

The lamps are ready and the door can be opened. Jesus pictures waiting that is attentive, then surprises His hearers with the master's welcome.

Read and discover

Lamps burning for a welcome

Jesus describes servants with lamps burning, waiting for a lord who is returning from a wedding. Their task is to be ready to open when he knocks. The scene gives watchfulness an attentive and welcoming character, rather than presenting it only as alarm about an approaching event. Luke 12:22-40

Luke 12:22-40

Read and discover

A master who serves

The waiting may extend into later watches of the night. The servants do not control the hour, but they can remain prepared for the master's arrival. The passage keeps uncertainty about timing beside clarity about their present responsibility.

The surprising promise is that the master will gird himself, have the servants sit down, and serve them. Jesus includes this reversal in the picture of the welcomed return. The servants' readiness is met with generosity that exceeds a routine account of duty.

Luke 12:22-40

Read and discover

Make the waiting attentive

You might reflect on the difference between waiting with attention and merely postponing life. Faithful care, prayer, and readiness to respond can belong to the interval itself. The image also invites us to receive the Lord's kindness rather than imagine discipleship solely as an endless demand to perform.

Pause and consider

  • What does a ready lamp suggest about your attention?
  • How does the master's service surprise you?
