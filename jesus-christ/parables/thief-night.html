The Householder and the Thief

The hour the householder does not know

Jesus uses the unexpected arrival of a thief to teach readiness. A householder who knew the watch in which a thief would come would stay awake to prevent the break-in. The difficulty is precisely that the hour is not known. Matthew 24:42-44

Matthew 24:42-44Luke 12:39-40

Surprise and preparation

The comparison moves from that uncertainty to the coming of the Son of Man. Its point concerns surprise and preparedness. It does not compare the Lord's character with a thief's dishonesty, and it does not invite readers to imagine that fear is the highest form of faith.

Luke places the saying beside servants awaiting their master's return and follows it with a discussion of entrusted responsibility. That setting helps keep readiness connected with how a person lives and serves, rather than with fascination about the time of arrival. Luke 12:39-40

What can be made ready today?

A useful reflection is to ask what would remain worth doing if the timetable stayed unknown. Faithful attention, reconciliation where possible, and care for an entrusted task can be practiced now. The passage calls for preparation without giving a warrant for speculation.

Pause and consider

  • What would faithful readiness look like today?
  • What changes when preparation is steady rather than alarmed?
