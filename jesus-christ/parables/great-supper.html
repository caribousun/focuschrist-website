A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Great Supper

The supper is ready, but the first invitees excuse themselves. The widening invitation brings overlooked people toward a place at the table.

Words about the kingdom at a meal

At a meal, a guest speaks of the blessedness of eating bread in God's kingdom. Jesus replies with a story about a man who prepares a great supper. When it is ready, his servant goes to summon the invited guests. The account asks how a warmly spoken idea becomes an actual response. Luke 14:12-24

Luke 14:12-24

Read and discover

Excuses and an expanding invitation

The invitees excuse themselves through a purchased field, newly bought oxen, and a marriage. Their differing reasons produce the same result: they do not come. The story does not require every ordinary responsibility to be treated as wrong; it exposes refusal of this invitation.

The host sends the servant into the streets to bring people ordinarily left at the margins. When room remains, the invitation extends farther. The movement is toward a filled house, with welcome reaching beyond those who first received the summons.

Luke 14:12-24

Read and discover

Consider both receiving and welcoming

This account should remain distinct from Matthew's royal wedding. Luke does not add a king's son or a wedding-garment encounter. Its particular force lies in the ready supper, the excuses, and the widening welcome.

Reflection can attend to both sides of the table. What makes it difficult for you to respond to a meaningful invitation? And when you are in a position to welcome others, who might need a place that is offered with dignity rather than as a display of your generosity?

Pause and consider

  • Which excuse feels familiar?
  • Whom might your table or community overlook?
