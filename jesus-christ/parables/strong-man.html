A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Strong Man

Jesus answers an accusation by asking how an existing hold can be broken. The comparison directs attention to His authority to deliver.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

An accusation against deliverance

The strong-man image answers a serious accusation. After Jesus delivers a person from an evil spirit, opponents attribute His power to the ruler of devils. Jesus first points out the contradiction of a kingdom fighting against itself. He then asks what must happen before a strong man's goods can be taken. Matthew 12:25-29

Matthew 12:25-29Mark 3:23-27Luke 11:17-22

Power that breaks a hold

In Matthew and Mark, the strong man must be bound before his house is plundered. Luke tells the comparison with a strong, armed guard and a stronger challenger who overcomes him. Each version directs attention to power sufficient to break the existing hold. Luke 11:17-22

The image helps explain Jesus' work of deliverance. He is not an ally of the evil He confronts. The comparison does not praise burglary or ask disciples to imitate an aggressor; it uses a recognizable contest of strength to answer the accusation about His authority.

Matthew 12:25-29Mark 3:23-27Luke 11:17-22

Look at the good He does

When studying this passage, begin with what Jesus has actually done for the person in need. His opponents' explanation obscures that act of deliverance. Reflection might ask whether a fixed opinion sometimes prevents us from recognizing goodness, help, or truth when it appears.

Pause and consider

  • How does the image answer the accusation?
  • What does deliverance reveal about His authority?
