The Wicked Tenants

A vineyard is entrusted to people who begin acting as though it belongs entirely to them. The story confronts rejection and accountability.

A vineyard entrusted to others

A vineyard owner prepares his property and entrusts it to tenants while he goes away. When the time comes to receive its fruit, he sends servants. Jesus tells the story amid a confrontation about His authority, and the Gospel accounts report that the leaders recognize its challenge to them. Matthew 21:23-46

Read and discover

Messengers rejected and the son sent

The tenants respond to successive servants with violence. The owner finally sends his son, but they treat even that arrival as an opportunity to seize the inheritance. The vineyard is not theirs, yet they act as though removing the owner's representatives will make it so. Mark 12:1-12

Jesus brings the hearers to the question of what the owner will do, then speaks of the rejected stone becoming the head of the corner. The account connects rejection with accountability. Luke likewise preserves both the tenants' actions and the leaders' recognition that Jesus has spoken against them. Luke 20:9-19

Reading the confrontation faithfully

The story must not become collective blame against Jewish people. Jesus, His disciples, and the scriptural world of the passage are Jewish; the confrontation is with particular leaders and their response. Careful study preserves that setting instead of extending hostility beyond the text.

For personal reflection, the vineyard can prompt questions about entrusted responsibility. What changes when a role, relationship, or opportunity is received as a stewardship rather than possessed as an unquestionable right? Listening to correction may be part of honoring the One to whom the vineyard belongs.

Pause and consider

  • What has been entrusted to your care?
  • How do you receive correction from a rightful source?
