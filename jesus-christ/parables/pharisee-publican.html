A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Pharisee and the Publican

Two people pray, but their posture toward God and others differs. Jesus invites a searching look at comparison, confidence, and mercy.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

Who trusts in being righteous?

Luke names the audience before telling the story: people who trust in their own righteousness and despise others. Two men go to the temple to pray, a Pharisee and a publican, or tax collector. The opening already directs attention toward confidence, comparison, and contempt. Luke 18:9-14

Luke 18:9-14

Read and discover

Two men pray

The Pharisee recounts his practices and distinguishes himself from people he considers worse, including the man nearby. The tax collector stands at a distance, will not lift his eyes to heaven, and asks God for mercy as a sinner.

Jesus says that the latter goes home justified rather than the former. His concluding statement about humility and self-exaltation explains the reversal. The issue is not that prayer, fasting, or giving are bad; it is the use of religious practice as grounds for superiority before God.

Luke 18:9-14

Read and discover

Let the comparison turn inward

The story can be misread if we congratulate ourselves for being unlike the Pharisee. That would reproduce the comparison it exposes. A more searching response is to examine our own prayer: are we presenting a case for our rank, or coming honestly before God with need and gratitude?

Pause and consider

  • When does comparison enter your prayer?
  • What would an honest request for mercy sound like?
