A comparison taught by Jesus Christ

A Parent's Good Gifts

A child's request for food becomes a way to think about prayer. Jesus directs trust toward the Father's goodness.

Read and discover

A hungry child's request

Jesus invites His hearers to ask, seek, and knock, then turns to a parent's response to a hungry child. If a child asks for bread, a stone is not a fitting answer; if the request is for fish, a serpent is not a good substitute. The contrast is deliberately plain. Matthew 7:7-12

Matthew 7:7-12Luke 11:11-13

Read and discover

From human care to the Father's goodness

The argument moves from imperfect human care toward the Father's greater goodness. Luke's account names the Holy Ghost as the gift the Father gives to those who ask. The passage therefore directs trust toward God's character, not merely toward the act of repeating a request. Luke 11:11-13

Some readers have experienced parents who did not protect or nourish them. This comparison need not require denying that experience. Jesus points toward the goodness of the Father by naming what a caring response should be, rather than declaring that every earthly parent has acted that way.

Matthew 7:7-12Luke 11:11-13

Read and discover

Ask with honest trust

Prayer can be honest about both need and uncertainty. You might consider what it means to seek a good gift while leaving room for an answer you cannot yet describe. The passage encourages confidence in the Father without promising every desired outcome in the form or timing requested.

Pause and consider

  • What does the contrast reveal about God?
  • What gift can you seek without dictating its timing?
