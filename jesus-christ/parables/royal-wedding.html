A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Wedding of the King's Son

The wedding invitation is refused, widened, and finally answered. Matthew's account asks readers to take both the welcome and their response seriously.

Read and discover

The invitation is ready

A king prepares a wedding for his son and sends servants to summon invited guests. Their refusal is followed by another invitation announcing that everything is ready. Some go to their own affairs; others mistreat and kill the servants. The story gives the rejection serious weight. Matthew 22:1-14

Matthew 22:1-14

Read and discover

Refusal, welcome, and the garment

The king then sends servants to the roads to gather people they find. The wedding fills with guests described as both bad and good. The widening invitation is a central movement, but the account does not end at the doorway. The king enters and addresses a guest without wedding clothing.

That final exchange prevents a reading in which receiving an invitation makes every response irrelevant. At the same time, the text does not itself supply a detailed history of how wedding garments were distributed. An explanation should not turn an unverified custom into the key on which the whole passage depends.

Matthew 22:1-14

Read and discover

An invitation that calls for response

Matthew's story is distinct from Luke's great supper. The king's son, the violence, and the garment episode belong here and should not be silently inserted into Luke. Keeping the accounts separate allows each to ask its own questions about invitation, refusal, and response.

You might consider what draws your attention away from an invitation that matters. Reflection can also ask how preparation expresses respect for what has been offered. The passage is a summons to a serious response, not permission to decide which present-day people belong outside the feast.

Pause and consider

  • What competes with a meaningful invitation?
  • How might receiving a gift also call for preparation?
