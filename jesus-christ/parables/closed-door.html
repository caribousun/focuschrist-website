The Closed Door

People outside appeal to having been near the master. Jesus' picture asks whether familiarity has become a faithful response.

A question about how many

Someone asks Jesus whether few will be saved. His response turns from speculation about a number toward the hearers' own response. The image of a householder who has risen and shut the door gives that call urgency. People outside begin to knock and ask admission. Luke 13:23-30

Luke 13:23-30

Read and discover

Familiarity is not the whole response

They appeal to familiarity: they have eaten and drunk in his presence, and he has taught in their streets. The reply does not accept proximity as a substitute for belonging. The account makes a distinction between having been near the teaching and having responded to it.

The surrounding verses picture people coming from different directions to sit in God's kingdom and speak of the last and the first. This widening horizon resists a comfortable assumption that one's current place or association settles the question.

Luke 13:23-30

Read and discover

Answer the invitation before you

You might ask where knowledge about Christ has yet to become a response to Him. The passage calls for seriousness without authorizing you to pronounce a neighbor's final standing. Its question can be received personally: what invitation is open before you now, and what would answering it mean?

Pause and consider

  • Where might familiarity substitute for response?
  • What invitation can you answer now?
