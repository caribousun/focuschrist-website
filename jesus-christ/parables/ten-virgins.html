A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Ten Virgins

All ten wait and all ten sleep, but their preparation differs. The midnight announcement reveals what they have brought to the waiting.

Read and discover

A wait longer than expected

Ten virgins take their lamps and go to meet a bridegroom. Five bring additional oil and five do not. The story belongs to Jesus' teaching about watchfulness, but the waiting itself becomes longer than expected. All ten grow drowsy and sleep. Matthew 25:1-13

Matthew 25:1-13

Read and discover

The midnight call and the lamps

The midnight announcement wakes them all. They rise to prepare their lamps, and the lack of oil becomes urgent. The distinction is not that the wise never slept. Their earlier preparation enables them to respond when the moment arrives.

While the others go to buy oil, the bridegroom comes and the prepared attendants enter the marriage feast. The closed door gives the ending its seriousness. Jesus concludes with the call to watch because the day and hour are not known.

Matthew 25:1-13

Read and discover

Preparation cannot be borrowed

The account should not be stretched into a rule against sharing material help. Its central concern is a readiness that cannot simply be transferred at the last moment. Nor does it justify calculating the return's date from imagined meanings in the lamps or hours.

As an application, some forms of preparation grow through repeated personal response: turning to God, learning His words, and acting on them. Another person's faithfulness can encourage you, but it cannot make every response on your behalf. You might choose one small practice that helps you prepare steadily rather than fearfully.

Pause and consider

  • What preparation grows through repeated choices?
  • What can you begin without fear or comparison?
