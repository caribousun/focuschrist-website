A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Rich Fool

A rich harvest promises the owner many comfortable years. The story asks what possessions can and cannot secure.

An inheritance question

Someone in the crowd asks Jesus to tell a brother to divide an inheritance. Jesus does not take up the requested role of divider. He warns about covetousness and tells a story in which abundant possessions cannot provide the security their owner expects. Luke 12:13-21

Luke 12:13-21

Read and discover

The future the barns cannot secure

The land of a rich man produces plentifully. Faced with more than he can store, he plans to tear down his barns and build larger ones. His imagined future centers on goods laid up for many years and the ease those goods will allow.

The interruption is abrupt: his life will be required that night. The stored possessions cannot keep the future he has planned within his control. Jesus concludes by contrasting treasure laid up for oneself with being rich toward God.

Luke 12:13-21

Read and discover

What does being rich toward God change?

The story does not say the crop was dishonestly gained, nor does it condemn every act of saving or practical preparation. Its challenge concerns the place possessions occupy in the man's understanding of life. Reflection might ask what you expect money to secure and whose needs disappear when everything is arranged only around your own ease.

Pause and consider

  • What does enough mean to you?
  • What would being rich toward God change about a current choice?
