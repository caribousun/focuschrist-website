A comparison taught by Jesus Christ

The Sheep in the Pit

The rescue of a sheep helps expose the good of helping a person. Jesus brings mercy into the Sabbath dispute.

Read and discover

The rescue everyone understands

During a dispute about healing on the Sabbath, Jesus asks what a person would do if a sheep fell into a pit. The expected rescue exposes a practical care His hearers already recognize. He then turns attention to the greater value of a person. Matthew 12:9-13

Matthew 12:9-13Luke 14:1-6

Read and discover

The person before the rule

The argument reaches a clear conclusion: it is lawful to do good on the Sabbath. Jesus' comparison is not separated from the person before Him whose hand needs healing. Mercy is not an abstract exception considered at a safe distance from need.

Luke records a related question about an ass or ox fallen into a pit while Jesus heals a man. These are related comparisons in distinct accounts; a retelling should not blend the animals and encounters into one event. Each connects recognized care with a challenge to resistance toward healing. Luke 14:1-6

Let compassion clarify the good

You might reflect on whether a concern for correct observance sometimes prevents you from seeing the person the observance should help you love. Careful practice and compassion need not be enemies. In this passage Jesus brings the good of the person clearly into view.

Pause and consider

  • What need might compassion help you see?
  • When can rules distract from doing good?
