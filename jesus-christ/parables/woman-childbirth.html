Sorrow Turning to Joy

Jesus acknowledges the disciples' approaching sorrow and promises reunion. A childbirth comparison allows pain and coming joy to appear in the same teaching.

Sorrow in the farewell discourse

In His farewell discourse, Jesus tells the disciples that they will weep and be sorrowful while the world rejoices, but that their sorrow will turn to joy. He gives the image of a woman in labor to describe a real anguish followed by a changed moment. John 16:16-24

John 16:16-24

Read and discover

The promised change and reunion

The woman's sorrow is not dismissed as imaginary. The birth brings joy that changes her relation to the pain she has endured. Jesus then returns directly to the disciples, saying He will see them again and that their hearts will rejoice.

This is a comparison within a particular promise to His followers as His death and their reunion approach. It is not a general prediction that every pregnancy ends safely or that every grief will soon feel resolved. Those conclusions would exceed what the passage says.

John 16:16-24

Read and discover

Hope that does not erase pain

You may reflect on how hope can remain present without requiring denial of sorrow. The disciples are not told that they will never weep. The image allows the present pain to be named while directing attention toward the reunion Jesus promises them.

Pause and consider

  • How does the image acknowledge real sorrow?
  • How can hope accompany pain?
