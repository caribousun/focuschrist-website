A parable taught by Jesus Christ

Choosing the Lower Place

At a meal, Jesus notices the places people choose. His teaching moves from seeking honor to offering generous welcome.

The seats people choose

Jesus notices how guests choose places of honor at a meal. His teaching grows from something happening in front of Him. He describes the embarrassment of taking a high place that must later be given to a more distinguished guest. Luke 14:1-14

Luke 14:1-14

Read and discover

Humility and hospitality

The alternative is to take the lower place and allow the host to invite one forward. The story culminates in a saying about self-exaltation and humility. Its purpose is deeper than providing an efficient technique for gaining public recognition.

Jesus then turns to the person who invited Him. He speaks of welcoming those who cannot repay the invitation, including people often excluded from honored tables. The movement from seating oneself to choosing one's guests joins humility with generosity.

Luke 14:1-14

Read and discover

A welcome without repayment

You might ask whose approval determines your sense of place, and who could receive welcome without increasing your status. A meal, gathering, or act of hospitality can become less concerned with repayment. The invitation is to honor people rather than arrange them according to what they can return.

Pause and consider

  • When are you tempted to secure visible honor?
  • Who could receive hospitality without being able to repay?
