A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Servant's Duty

Completed service is not leverage over God. Jesus' household picture challenges entitlement while calling disciples toward faithfulness.

Faith, forgiveness, and service

After teaching about forgiveness and responding to the Apostles' request for increased faith, Jesus describes a servant returning from work. The picture belongs to a world in which a servant might labor in the field and then attend the household meal. Luke 17:1-10

Luke 17:1-10

Read and discover

Duty without leverage

The servant does not return expecting the master immediately to serve him. He prepares the meal and completes the assigned work. Jesus uses that familiar expectation to challenge the idea that fulfilling a duty puts the master under an obligation to the servant.

The conclusion concerns the disciples' posture after doing what they have been commanded. Their obedience is not leverage by which they force God to repay them. The language should not be detached from that point and used to deny their worth as people.

Luke 17:1-10

Read and discover

Serve without making God a debtor

A present-day application might examine resentment when service goes unnoticed. Can gratitude and love sustain an act that receives no applause? That question does not excuse exploitation or remove the need for rest and fair treatment; it asks what attitude we bring to faithful service before God.

Pause and consider

  • When does service become a demand for recognition?
  • What would grateful faithfulness look like?
