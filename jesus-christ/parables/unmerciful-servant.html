A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Unmerciful Servant

A servant receives compassion and then refuses it to another. Jesus asks how mercy received should change the way we respond.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

Peter asks about forgiveness

Peter asks Jesus how often he must forgive a brother who sins against him. Jesus answers with a story about a king settling accounts. One servant owes an immense sum and cannot pay. Faced with consequences that reach his whole household, he begs for patience. Matthew 18:21-35

Matthew 18:21-35

Read and discover

A debt released and mercy refused

The king does more than extend the deadline: moved with compassion, he forgives the debt. The servant then encounters a fellow servant who owes him far less. Despite hearing a plea resembling his own, he seizes the man and has him imprisoned. The contrast makes his refusal of mercy unmistakable.

Other servants are distressed and report what happened. The king's question brings the central issue into the open: should the man who received compassion not also have shown it? Jesus concludes by directing attention to forgiveness from the heart. The forgiven debt is meant to change the way the servant treats others.

Matthew 18:21-35

Read and discover

Receiving mercy changes the question

The parable does not make cruelty harmless or demand that a wounded person restore unsafe access. Its call is to relinquish a merciless posture while taking wrongdoing seriously. Reflection can begin with the difference between necessary boundaries and the desire to keep another person permanently under your judgment.

Forgiveness may involve difficult and repeated work. A reader carrying deep injury need not pretend that the feeling has already changed. The account invites an honest turning toward the mercy of God and a willingness to consider what receiving that mercy might make possible in one's own heart.

Pause and consider

  • Where are you keeping a harsh ledger?
  • How might compassion change one response while preserving needed boundaries?
