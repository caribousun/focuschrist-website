A comparison taught by Jesus Christ

Salt of the Earth

The image of salt asks whether discipleship retains the qualities that give it meaning. Read the saying in its several Gospel settings.

What gives salt its use?

Jesus tells His disciples that they are the salt of the earth. The comparison asks what use salt has if it loses its savor. Its point rests on the difference between a quality that makes something useful and an outward form from which that quality is missing. Matthew 5:13-16

The image in different settings

Mark places salt near teaching about conduct within the disciples' community and concludes with being at peace with one another. Luke uses the loss of savor after teaching about the cost of discipleship. Those settings give the image distinct emphases while preserving its concern with meaningful character. Mark 9:49-50

The saying should not become a debate that forgets why Jesus uses the comparison. Nor should it become a declaration that some people have no worth. It challenges disciples to consider whether their life carries the qualities to which He calls them. Luke 14:34-35

Preserve what gives service meaning

As an application, ask what gives a particular act of service its value. Attention, honesty, and care may matter more than making the act visible. Mark's connection with peace also invites reflection on whether your way of defending a good thing preserves the spirit of the teaching you defend.

Pause and consider

  • What gives your service its value?
  • Why does Mark connect salt with peace?
