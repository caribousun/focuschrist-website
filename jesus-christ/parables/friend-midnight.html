A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Friend at Midnight

An unexpected guest leaves a host asking for bread in the night. Jesus places persistence beside the Father's goodness.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

A guest arrives and bread is needed

After teaching His disciples to pray, Jesus describes a host whose friend arrives from a journey at midnight. The host has nothing to set before the guest, so he goes to another friend's house and asks for three loaves. The need arises through the desire to welcome someone else. Luke 11:1-13

Luke 11:1-13

Read and discover

The request beyond the closed door

The friend inside says the door is shut and his children are with him in bed. The request is inconvenient. Yet the story moves toward the bread being provided, emphasizing persistence in bringing the need rather than retreating at the first obstacle.

Jesus follows the account with invitations to ask, seek, and knock, then speaks of the Father's good gifts. Those surrounding verses guard against treating God as an irritated neighbor who must be worn down. The movement is toward confidence in the Father, not distrust of His willingness to care.

Luke 11:1-13

Read and discover

Keep turning toward the Father

You can bring a need honestly, including a need you carry for someone else. Persistent prayer need not be polished or accompanied by certainty about an outcome. The passage invites continued turning toward God while leaving the timing and form of the answer in His hands.

Pause and consider

  • What need can you bring honestly to God?
  • How does the Father's generosity change the way you ask?
