A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Sheep and the Goats

The King remembers meals, visits, clothing, and welcome. This judgment scene places ordinary acts of care within our response to Christ.

Before the Son of Man

Matthew closes this teaching sequence with the Son of Man seated in glory and the nations gathered before Him. A shepherd separating sheep from goats supplies the opening comparison. The passage then becomes an extended judgment scene in which the King addresses those before Him. Matthew 25:31-46

Matthew 25:31-46

Read and discover

The needs the King remembers

The acts He names are concrete: feeding the hungry, giving drink, welcoming a stranger, clothing someone, caring for the sick, and visiting a prisoner. Those addressed are surprised. They ask when they saw Him in those conditions.

The King's answer joins their treatment of people in need to their treatment of Him. The corresponding failure to care is also taken seriously. The passage refuses to let devotion remain detached from the person who needs food, company, clothing, or welcome.

Matthew 25:31-46

Read and discover

Care offered to Him

This is not an invitation to turn another person's hardship into a chance to advertise our goodness. Nor does a checklist authorize us to announce anyone's final judgment. The needs in the account belong to real people whose dignity matters.

You might begin with one need that is within your ability to meet responsibly. A visit, a meal, or attentive companionship can be a meaningful act without being a complete solution to suffering. The study invites us to recognize service to Christ in care for those before us.

Pause and consider

  • Whose ordinary need can you notice?
  • How does seeing service as offered to Christ alter your attention?
