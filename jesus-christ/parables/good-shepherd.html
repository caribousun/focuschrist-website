An allegory taught by Jesus Christ

The Good Shepherd and the Door

The shepherd knows, calls, leads, and gives His life. Jesus identifies Himself through a sustained picture of care and willing sacrifice.

Begin the study ↓

Jesus develops the image

Jesus speaks of sheep, a doorway, a shepherd, strangers, and a hired worker after the account of a man born blind and the controversy surrounding his healing. His hearers do not at first understand the figure, so He develops it by naming Himself as the door and the good shepherd. John 10:1-21

John 10:1-21

Read and discover

A voice known and a life freely given

The shepherd calls his own sheep by name and leads them out. They follow because they know his voice. The picture includes recognition and relationship, not merely movement within a crowd. The shepherd's knowledge of the flock gives direction a personal character.

The hired worker abandons the sheep when danger comes because they are not his own. Jesus contrasts that flight with the good shepherd's willingness to lay down his life. He then speaks of laying down His life and taking it again, giving the image a direct connection with His saving mission.

John 10:1-21

Read and discover

The gathering beyond this fold

He also speaks of other sheep who will hear His voice and of one flock under one shepherd. A full study should retain this outward movement rather than use the image only for private reassurance. His care is personal and His gathering reaches beyond the immediate fold.

The Bible Dictionary classifies this teaching as an allegory, while the Harmony uses the broader label of parable. That difference in study terminology does not diminish the passage. Unlike a character in an ordinary parable, Jesus explicitly identifies Himself within this extended teaching image.

John 10:1-21

Read and discover

Learning the Shepherd's voice

You might consider how you come to recognize a trustworthy voice. In this passage trust is connected with knowledge, care, and willing sacrifice. Following the Savior invites learning His words and character, rather than treating every strong impression or confident speaker as His voice.

Pause and consider

  • What marks a trustworthy voice?
  • How does willing sacrifice reveal His care?
