A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Wheat and the Tares

The servants want to pull out what does not belong. The owner's answer asks them to consider what their haste might also destroy.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

A field with mixed growth

Jesus tells of a field deliberately planted with good seed. An enemy introduces tares while people sleep, and the mixed growth becomes apparent later. The servants' first question is understandable: how did the unwanted plants appear in a field that began with good seed? Matthew 13:24-43

Matthew 13:24-43

Read and discover

Why the owner tells them to wait

Their proposed solution is immediate removal. The owner refuses because pulling up the tares could uproot the wheat as well. He directs them to let both grow until harvest, when separation can take place. His restraint protects what the servants are trying to save.

After the crowd leaves, the disciples ask Jesus to explain. He identifies the sower of good seed as the Son of Man, the field as the world, and the harvest with the end. Because the passage supplies these meanings, they should guide the study rather than an imagined code attached to every detail.

Matthew 13:24-43

Read and discover

Care without claiming final judgment

The account cautions against acting as though we possess the final discernment of the Lord. It does not ask us to call our neighbors weeds or excuse harmful conduct. A personal reflection can focus on the patience, care, and humility needed to protect people while leaving final judgment to Him.

Pause and consider

  • Why does the owner delay separation?
  • How can patience protect someone vulnerable?
