A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Seed Growing Secretly

Between sowing and harvest, growth unfolds beyond the farmer's control. Mark's brief parable invites patient attention to God's kingdom.

Begin the study ↓

Growth beyond the farmer's command

Mark records a kingdom comparison found only in his Gospel. A person casts seed into the ground, then continues through nights and days while the seed grows. The farmer participates in the process, yet does not command or fully explain its unfolding. Jesus lets that limit remain in the picture. Mark 4:26-29

Mark 4:26-29

Read and discover

From sowing to harvest

The growth has a sequence: blade, ear, and full grain. Harvest follows when the crop is ready. Waiting is therefore neither emptiness nor an abandonment of the field. Much is happening between sowing and reaping, even when the sower cannot make it happen faster.

This story differs from the wheat and tares. There is no enemy planting weeds and no proposal to pull them out. Its attention rests on growth and its appointed ripeness. Combining the two stories would hide the particular contribution of Mark's account.

Mark 4:26-29

Read and discover

Faithful effort and patient trust

Faithful effort can coexist with limited control. You might consider a good responsibility you are tending whose results are not yet visible. The parable invites patience, while the sowing and harvest remind us that patience still has work to do.

Pause and consider

  • What can you faithfully tend without controlling the result?
  • What small growth might you overlook?
