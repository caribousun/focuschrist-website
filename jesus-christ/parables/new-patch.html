A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The New Patch

A damaged garment helps Jesus answer a question about fasting. The small comparison asks His hearers to understand His ministry before forcing it into familiar expectations.

The question about fasting

A question about fasting gives this small picture its setting. John's disciples ask why they and the Pharisees fast while Jesus' disciples do not. Jesus first speaks of the joy of wedding guests while the bridegroom is with them, then turns to the familiar work of repairing clothing. Matthew 9:14-17

Matthew 9:14-17Mark 2:21Luke 5:36

A repair that deepens the tear

A piece of new cloth does not make a sound repair simply because it covers the hole. When it pulls against an old garment, the damage becomes worse. Luke sharpens the picture by describing a piece taken from a new garment: the attempted repair harms what is new and does not match what is old. Luke 5:36

The comparison belongs to Jesus' answer about His ministry. He is asking His hearers to understand what is happening in His presence, rather than force His disciples into an expectation without considering its setting. The picture's practical logic matters more than an invented symbolic meaning for every thread.

Receiving what Jesus is teaching

There can be a place for examining how we receive unfamiliar teaching. Do we listen long enough to understand it, or immediately trim it to fit an existing habit? That reflection should remain humble: Jesus' image does not mean that everything old is worthless or every novelty is true.

Pause and consider

  • What question prompted this picture?
  • Where might habit keep you from listening carefully?
