A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Talents

Different amounts are entrusted to different servants. The returning master asks what each has done with the responsibility received.

What the master entrusts

A man going away entrusts property to his servants. One receives five talents, another two, and another one, each according to ability. In the story a talent is a large monetary amount. The familiar modern use of the word for personal gifts can be an application, but it is not the original object being distributed. Matthew 25:14-30

Matthew 25:14-30

Read and discover

Different amounts, faithful work

The first two servants work with what they receive and increase it. The third digs in the ground and hides his master's money. Their different starting amounts do not prevent the first two from being described through the same pattern of faithful action.

After a long time, the master returns and settles accounts. The first and second servants each report what they have done and receive the same welcoming commendation. The story does not make equal starting resources the condition for faithful service.

Matthew 25:14-30

Read and discover

Fear and the buried trust

The third servant explains his inaction through fear and his view of the master. He returns what he hid but has done nothing productive with the trust. The exchange exposes the consequence of allowing fear to govern the whole stewardship.

This account should remain distinct from Luke's ten pounds. The number of servants, initial distributions, surrounding events, and details of the accounting differ. Keeping those differences allows readers to hear Matthew's story rather than a blended version.

Matthew 25:14-30

Read and discover

Use what is actually in your hands

A present-day reflection may consider resources, ability, time, or opportunities that have actually been entrusted to you. The invitation is faithful use, not comparison with someone else's amount. It is also not a promise of investment returns: the spiritual question concerns your response to trust and responsibility.

Pause and consider

  • What has actually been entrusted to you?
  • How might fear be keeping one responsibility buried?
