A comparison taught by Jesus Christ

A Divided Kingdom and House

An accusation fails to explain the deliverance before the hearers. Jesus exposes the contradiction through a kingdom and household divided.

The claim against Jesus

Opponents claim that Jesus casts out devils through the ruler of devils. He replies by considering what happens when a kingdom or household is divided against itself. Internal opposition undermines the very power the accusation claims He is serving. Matthew 12:25-26

Matthew 12:25-26Mark 3:23-26Luke 11:17-18

Read and discover

An explanation that contradicts the work

Mark records Jesus asking how Satan can cast out Satan, then applies the divided-kingdom and divided-house comparisons. His reasoning calls attention to the work actually being done. A person is being delivered from evil, and the proposed explanation does not fit that deliverance. Mark 3:23-26

Luke preserves the same answer before developing the strong-man comparison. The two images belong to the argument but contribute different parts: division exposes the contradiction, and the stronger challenger explains the breaking of an existing hold. Luke 11:17-18

Matthew 12:25-26Mark 3:23-26Luke 11:17-18

Judge a claim with attention

The passage can encourage careful judgment about claims and their evidence. A memorable accusation should not replace attention to what has happened. Its household image is not a command to remain in an unsafe relationship for the sake of appearances; the immediate subject is the charge against Jesus' deliverance.

Pause and consider

  • What contradiction does He expose?
  • How does His actual work answer the accusation?
