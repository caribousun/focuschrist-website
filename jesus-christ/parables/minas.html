A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Ten Pounds or Minas

People expect the kingdom immediately; Jesus tells of work during an absence. Luke's account joins waiting with stewardship.

Why the kingdom does not appear immediately

Luke explains why Jesus tells this parable: He is near Jerusalem, and people suppose the kingdom of God will appear immediately. A nobleman who travels to receive a kingdom and then return provides a story about an interval, entrusted work, and later accountability. Luke 19:11-27

Luke 19:11-27

Read and discover

Work during the nobleman's absence

The nobleman gives ten servants money and directs them to put it to use. Citizens who oppose him send a message rejecting his rule. When he returns, he calls the servants to learn what they have gained. The servants' work has taken place during the contested and uncertain interval.

One reports ten pounds gained and another five. A further servant has kept the pound wrapped in a cloth, explaining his inaction through fear. The account examines what the servants do with the trust, not merely whether they retain possession of the original coin.

Luke 19:11-27

Read and discover

The account at his return

The nobleman orders the unused pound taken from the servant and given to the one who has ten. The closing words also command that the opponents who rejected his rule be brought before him and slain. Luke records that command without narrating its execution; these severe features belong to the story Jesus tells, not to instructions for His hearers to harm others.

The nobleman's harsh features should not be treated as a complete portrait of God. A parable compares particular aspects of a story with a teaching; it does not require every action of every character to be imitated or assigned directly to the Savior.

Keep this account distinct from Matthew's talents. Luke includes ten servants, equal initial pounds, opposition to the nobleman, and authority over cities. Its opening specifically challenges the expectation of an immediate manifestation of the kingdom.

Luke 19:11-27

Faithfulness during the interval

As an application, consider what faithful work belongs to a season of waiting. Uncertainty about a larger outcome does not erase the responsibility that is already in your hands. The story invites attention to that responsibility while resisting both passivity and speculative timetables.

Pause and consider

  • What would faithful work during waiting look like?
  • What fear might lead you to hide a responsibility?
