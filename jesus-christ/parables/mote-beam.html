A comparison taught by Jesus Christ

The Mote and the Beam

A speck and a beam expose the distortion of hypocritical judgment. Clearer help begins with truthful self-examination.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

An impossible obstruction

Jesus places a tiny speck beside a wooden beam to expose the absurdity of hypocritical judgment. A person notices a small obstruction in someone else's eye while failing to consider a much larger one in his own. The exaggerated picture makes self-deception difficult to miss. Matthew 7:1-5

Matthew 7:1-5Luke 6:41-42

Read and discover

Clear sight before correction

The instruction does not end by saying that no one should ever help another person. Jesus tells the hearer to remove the beam first, so that he can see clearly to help with the speck. Honest self-examination changes the manner and credibility of that help.

Luke preserves the same contrast amid teaching about mercy, judgment, and the influence of teachers. Read in that setting, the image asks for more than a temporary pause before criticism. It calls for a truthful view of oneself and a less condemning posture toward others. Luke 6:41-42

Matthew 7:1-5Luke 6:41-42

Read and discover

Begin with honest self-examination

You might notice a fault that quickly provokes you, then ask what your own response reveals. The goal is not self-hatred or pretending that harm does not matter. It is clearer sight, through which a needed conversation can become more humble, accurate, and compassionate.

Pause and consider

  • What fault do you readily notice in others?
  • What self-examination could make your help gentler?
