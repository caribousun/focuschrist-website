A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Lamp on a Stand

A lamp brings light into the room. Follow this recurring image through Jesus' teaching about witness, attention, and what a person receives within.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

A light meant to be seen

A lamp is useful because its light reaches beyond itself. Jesus returns to this ordinary household image in several settings. In Matthew, He speaks of the light His disciples should show through good works. In Mark and Luke, the lamp also appears beside teaching about what people hear and how they receive it. Matthew 5:14-16

Matthew 5:14-16Mark 4:21-25Luke 8:16-18Luke 11:33-36

Read and discover

Listening and shining

Mark asks whether a lamp is brought in to be hidden beneath a basket or bed. Luke describes placing it where those entering can see. The absurdity of concealing a useful light makes the teaching memorable: what has been received is not meant to remain ineffective. Mark 4:21-25

The accounts should be read together without erasing their different emphases. Matthew directs attention from good works to the Father. Luke's later saying moves toward the condition of the eye and the light within a person. The same familiar object can illuminate more than one aspect of discipleship. Luke 11:33-36

Matthew 5:14-16Mark 4:21-25Luke 8:16-18Luke 11:33-36

Read and discover

Let goodness reach another person

Sharing light need not mean drawing attention to yourself. A patient explanation, an honest act, or a needed kindness may help another person see goodness more clearly. You can also ask how attentively you are receiving the light you hope to share.

Pause and consider

  • What changes when the lamp is covered?
  • How could your conduct help someone see goodness?
