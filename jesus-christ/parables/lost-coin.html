A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Lost Coin

A lamp, a broom, and a careful search show that one missing coin still matters. Its recovery calls neighbors into the joy.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

One piece still matters

A woman has ten silver coins and loses one. Jesus tells her story between the lost sheep and the returning son, answering complaints that He receives sinners. The domestic search belongs to the same movement toward recovery and shared joy. Luke 15:1-10

Luke 15:1-10

Read and discover

Light, effort, and shared rejoicing

She lights a lamp, sweeps the house, and searches diligently until she finds the coin. These actions give attention a visible form. What is missing is not dismissed because nine remain; the search continues because the lost piece still matters.

On finding it, she calls friends and neighbors to rejoice. Jesus connects that celebration with joy in the presence of God's angels over one sinner who repents. The explanation supplied in the passage should remain central rather than an invented account of the woman's marriage or possessions.

Luke 15:1-10

Read and discover

Celebrate another person's return

The coin's recovery invites us to consider whether our response to another person's return includes genuine gladness. We may also notice the care of the search itself. Attention, effort, and welcome can communicate value more clearly than a statement that someone ought to come back.

Pause and consider

  • What does careful searching show about value?
  • How can you share joy over another person's return?
