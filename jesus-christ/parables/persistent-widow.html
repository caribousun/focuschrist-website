A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Persistent Widow and the Judge

A widow keeps bringing a claim that a judge dismisses. Jesus directs weary hearers toward continued prayer and the goodness of God.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

A prayer that continues

Luke introduces the story by stating its purpose: people should keep praying and not lose heart. A widow repeatedly comes to a judge seeking justice against an adversary. The judge is described as neither fearing God nor regarding people, so he is not introduced as a model of divine character. Luke 18:1-8

Luke 18:1-8

Read and discover

The widow and an unjust judge

For a time he refuses. Eventually he acts because her continued coming troubles him. The story gives persistence a concrete setting in a person whose claim has been dismissed, but it does not praise the judge's indifference.

Jesus draws attention to what even this unjust judge says, then turns toward God's response to His chosen people who cry to Him. The movement is a contrast, not an assertion that God must be irritated into concern. The closing question turns toward the presence of faith when the Son of Man comes.

Luke 18:1-8

Read and discover

Trust the God Jesus points toward

Readers who have waited long for justice may hear the opening invitation with particular weight. Continued prayer can include weariness, grief, and plain speech. The account does not supply a timetable for a specific legal case or promise that every requested outcome will occur immediately.

You might consider how to remain in prayer while also supporting practical efforts toward justice and care. Another person's long wait is not evidence of weak faith. The invitation is to persist without losing sight of the difference between an indifferent judge and the God to whom Jesus directs us.

Pause and consider

  • What can you continue bringing to God honestly?
  • How can you support someone waiting for justice?
