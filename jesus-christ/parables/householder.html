A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Householder's Treasure

After understanding comes the task of bringing learning forward. Jesus closes His kingdom comparisons with a householder's old and new treasures.

After the disciples understand

At the close of the kingdom parables, Jesus asks His disciples whether they have understood. Their reply leads to one final household comparison: a scribe instructed in the kingdom brings out treasure that is both new and old. The image is connected to learning and understanding. Matthew 13:51-53

Matthew 13:51-53

Read and discover

Treasures brought forward

The householder has resources to bring out, rather than merely objects to admire in storage. Jesus does not list the individual treasures or assign a separate meaning to each. The teaching can remain rich without turning the contents of the house into an invented symbolic inventory.

The pairing of new and old resists a careless assumption that understanding requires either discarding everything previously learned or refusing everything unfamiliar. The emphasis rests on a person instructed in the kingdom who can draw upon what has been received.

Matthew 13:51-53

Read and discover

Learning that can help another

As an application, consider how study equips you to help another person understand. An older passage may become newly meaningful beside something you have just learned. The question is not how much information you can display, but how faithfully and usefully you can bring it forward.

Pause and consider

  • How can older understanding help you receive something new?
  • What can you share faithfully from what you have learned?
