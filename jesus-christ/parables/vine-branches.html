The True Vine and the Branches

A branch lives through its connection to the vine. Jesus joins abiding, love, obedience, and fruitfulness in His farewell teaching.

The vine, the Father, and the disciples

During His farewell teaching, Jesus names Himself as the true vine and His Father as the husbandman. The disciples are the branches. The picture begins with dependence: a branch does not supply its own life independently of the vine to which it belongs. John 15:1-17

John 15:1-17

Read and discover

The life that bears fruit

The Father tends the growth, and Jesus invites the disciples to abide in Him. The repeated concern is fruitfulness. Remaining connected is not described as a decorative attachment; it is the relation through which the branch can bear fruit.

As the passage develops, abiding is joined with Jesus' words, His love, and keeping His commandments. He speaks of His joy and commands love for one another. Those surrounding teachings keep the vine from becoming a vague picture of feeling close without response.

John 15:1-17

Read and discover

Abiding in His love

Pruning is part of the image, but it should not be used to announce why a particular person has suffered. The passage does not authorize us to identify a bereavement, illness, or injury as a specific act of divine pruning. Its stated invitation is to abide in Christ.

For reflection, consider what helps His words remain present in your choices and relationships. Prayer, study, repentance, and acts of love can be considered as responses to that invitation. Fruit is received through dependence on Him, not offered as proof that a person needs no help.

Pause and consider

  • What helps you remain close to Him?
  • How do love and obedience belong together?
