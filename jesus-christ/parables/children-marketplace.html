A parable taught by Jesus Christ

Children in the Marketplace

When every invitation is refused, the problem may lie deeper than the invitation. Jesus uses children's play to uncover inconsistent resistance to John and Himself.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

Two ministries, two complaints

Jesus speaks about John the Baptist and the reactions both of them have received. John comes with an austere way of life and is accused of having a devil. Jesus eats and drinks with others and is called a glutton and a friend of sinners. The criticism changes, but the refusal remains. Matthew 11:7-19

Matthew 11:7-19Luke 7:31-35

The invitation always refused

Children in a marketplace give Him a picture for that response. They call to one another about music and mourning, but their companions will join neither. The image exposes a demand that others participate only on the critic's terms, followed by dissatisfaction whichever invitation is offered. Luke 7:31-35

Jesus does not ask His hearers to judge truth by a teacher's personality or by whether a messenger matches their preferred style. John and Jesus have different roles. Their difference does not excuse refusing both. The concluding reference to wisdom invites attention to what their ministries actually bring forth.

Listening beyond preference

This passage can turn us from quick dismissal toward honest listening. You might notice the standards you apply to a message you do not want to hear. Are those standards consistent, or do they shift so that no messenger could satisfy them?

Pause and consider

  • What remains constant in the two criticisms?
  • How can you recognize a message without demanding your preferred messenger?
