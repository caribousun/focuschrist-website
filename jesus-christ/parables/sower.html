A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Sower

The same seed meets different ground. Jesus explains what can prevent His word from taking root and what makes room for enduring fruit.

The crowd beside the water

A crowd gathers while Jesus teaches from a boat. He begins with a sower scattering seed, an action His hearers can readily picture. Later the disciples ask about the teaching. The Gospel accounts preserve both the story and Jesus' explanation, so the study should keep those two parts together. Matthew 13:1-23

What happens to the seed

Some seed falls along a path and is taken by birds. Some reaches shallow ground, sprouts quickly, and withers under heat. Other growth is crowded by thorns. Finally, seed in good soil bears a harvest. The movement is not simply from bad people to good people; it examines what happens to the received word. Mark 4:3-9,14-20

Jesus names obstacles to lasting fruit: failure to understand, tribulation that exposes a lack of root, and cares or riches that choke growth. Luke describes fruitful hearers as keeping the word and bringing forth fruit with patience. Initial enthusiasm and patient endurance are therefore different moments in receiving His teaching. Luke 8:4-8,11-15

Tending the ground of a life

The soils make space for honest self-examination without becoming permanent labels for ourselves or others. You may recognize more than one condition in your own life. A useful response is to identify one competing concern, one shallow habit, or one way to give the word more sustained attention.

Pause and consider

  • What currently competes for your attention?
  • What would help the word take deeper root?
