A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Laborers in the Vineyard

Equal generosity becomes difficult for workers who expected more. The vineyard story examines gratitude, comparison, and resentment.

A question about reward

The vineyard story follows Peter's question about what the disciples will receive after leaving everything to follow Jesus. Its opening and conclusion are framed by sayings about the first and the last. That context makes the account more than a lesson about an employer's unusual payment decision. Matthew 20:1-16

Matthew 20:1-16

Read and discover

Hiring, payment, and comparison

The householder hires workers early for an agreed wage, then returns at later hours to hire others. Some are still waiting near the end of the day because no one has hired them. The owner sends them into the vineyard as well. The text does not call the late workers lazy.

At payment, the last workers receive a full day's wage. Those hired first expect more, then resent receiving the amount they agreed to. The owner answers both their claim of unfairness and their reaction to his generosity. Nothing has been taken from them, yet another person's good has become hard to welcome.

Matthew 20:1-16

Read and discover

Can another person's good become your joy?

The story invites us to examine comparison in our response to grace. Do we measure another person's welcome against the time and effort of our own service? Gratitude can be undermined when blessing becomes a competition for position.

This is a kingdom parable, not a comprehensive rule for present-day employment. Its spiritual challenge remains searching: faithfulness does not require resentment toward someone whose opportunity or return came later. The invitation is to recognize generosity without treating it as a personal loss.

Pause and consider

  • When does another person's blessing feel like your loss?
  • What would gratitude notice about the owner's first agreement?
