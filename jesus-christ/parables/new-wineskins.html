A parable taught by Jesus Christ

New Wine and Wineskins

Wine needs a vessel able to receive it. Jesus uses this ordinary image to help His hearers consider the significance of His presence and teaching.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

The moment shapes the practice

Jesus places the wineskin comparison beside the image of a new patch on an old garment. Both answer a question about fasting. The surrounding conversation matters: His presence is likened to the bridegroom's presence with wedding guests, and He also speaks of a future time when the bridegroom will be taken away. Matthew 9:14-17

Matthew 9:14-17Mark 2:22Luke 5:37-39

Read and discover

Wine and a fitting vessel

New wine placed in old skins bursts them, losing both wine and container. New skins provide the fitting vessel. Jesus describes preservation, not merely destruction: the point of receiving the wine properly is that what is given can be kept. Mark and Luke retain the same central contrast. Mark 2:22

Luke adds that someone accustomed to old wine does not immediately desire new. That observation makes the passage more searching. Familiarity can shape what a person is willing to receive. It should not be used to overturn the comparison or announce that Jesus simply preferred the old order. Luke 5:37-39

Matthew 9:14-17Mark 2:22Luke 5:37-39

Becoming willing to receive

The picture invites careful receptiveness. You might ask whether an established expectation helps you recognize Christ's teaching or prevents you from hearing it. Receiving Him need not mean treating your past with contempt; it can mean allowing understanding and practice to change as you learn.

Pause and consider

  • How does this companion image strengthen the garment comparison?
  • What might readiness to learn require?
