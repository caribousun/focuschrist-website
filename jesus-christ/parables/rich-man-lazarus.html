A parable taught by Jesus Christ

The Rich Man and Lazarus

A suffering man lies at a rich man's gate. The account moves from that ignored need toward a call to hear the word already given.

Begin the study ↓

Read and discover

The need beside the gate

Jesus describes a rich man who dresses splendidly and feasts daily, while Lazarus lies in need at his gate. The contrast is close at hand rather than far away. The suffering person is visible within the rich man's ordinary surroundings. Luke 16:19-31

Luke 16:19-31

Read and discover

A reversal and a plea

Death changes the scene. Lazarus is carried to Abraham's bosom, while the rich man experiences torment. His request for relief receives an answer that recalls their former conditions and the gulf now between them.

The rich man then asks that Lazarus be sent to warn his brothers. Abraham directs attention to Moses and the prophets. The final exchange challenges the assumption that an extraordinary sign would produce the response that the hearers have refused to give to scripture.

Luke 16:19-31

Read and discover

Hear the word already given

The account should not be treated as a complete diagram of every aspect of life after death. Nor is this Lazarus identified as the man of Bethany whom Jesus raises. The story's moral force does not depend on merging those figures.

You might reflect first on the gate: who is near enough to notice, yet remains outside your attention? The ending also asks what you already know from God's word that calls for a response. Waiting for a more dramatic message can become a way of postponing an ordinary act of mercy.

Pause and consider

  • Who is close enough to see yet easy to ignore?
  • What existing teaching already calls for your response?
