The Leaven

A woman's ordinary work

A woman placing leaven into meal becomes Jesus' picture of the kingdom. Matthew gives the comparison in a single verse; Luke places it beside the mustard seed. Its brevity is part of its strength. An ordinary household action opens a way to think about a change that reaches beyond what is first visible. Matthew 13:33

Matthew 13:33Luke 13:20-21

Change throughout the meal

The woman hides the leaven in three measures of meal, and the whole is leavened. The story does not describe separate stages or give her a name. Its attention rests on the relation between the introduced leaven and the resulting change throughout the batch. Luke 13:20-21

Leaven does not carry one fixed moral meaning wherever it appears in scripture. Jesus also uses it in warnings elsewhere. In this passage He explicitly compares it with the kingdom, so the surrounding teaching determines how the image should be read.

The influence we cannot yet measure

You might consider the quiet influence of a sincere word, repeated kindness, or faithful example. That is a present-day application, not an additional explanation supplied in the verse. The picture invites attention to effects that may be real before they become easy to measure.

Pause and consider

  • What is unseen while the dough changes?
  • What quiet influence can you offer?
